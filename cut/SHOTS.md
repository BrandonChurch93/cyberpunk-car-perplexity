# Shot list

One shot per beat, in both formats. Beat times come from `beats` in the config and are quoted here only so the page can be read on its own. The camera code reads the config, not this file.

## Addendum 002

`kit/addenda/002-approved-look.md` set the hero camera. The wide beats (`arrival`, `wake`, `return`) in both formats are the cameras below and replace the round 8 draft for those beats. The macro beats keep their round 8 cameras, except `paint`: in both formats it moved to the right rear shoulder panel, on the mirror line of the billboard, so the panel carries the image of the billboard as `reference/keyframes/03-paint.jpg` does. No blockout critic judged these three; C5, C8 and C9 judge them in Phase 3, with light.

- The hero camera was fitted to `reference/approved/hero.jpg` from eight landmarks on the vehicle (both wheels on the near side and their contact points, both mirrors, the roof, the far front corner), for each listed focal length. 50 mm fitted best: 22 px rms on the 1672 px image against 30 px at 35 mm and 39 px at 24 mm.
- Fitted pose, master: lens at (3.15, 0.79, 7.87) m, heading 205.1 degrees (0 looks along +Z), 0.9 degrees down, 50 mm. The horizon lies on the parapet top.
- Where a line below disagrees with this section, this section wins: the billboard stands behind the vehicle right of center in the hero frame, the cyan sign group is at the camera's left and the amber group at its right, and the opening track is a straight push along the lens axis with no swing.
- Stop on the wide shots is now T2.8, so the near side of the vehicle holds from nose to rear wheel at 8.5 m.

## Conventions

- World: meters, Y up. The vehicle rests on y = 0 at the origin and faces +Z. Its left side is +X.
- The bay grid of the deck is turned 12 degrees against the vehicle, so the vehicle sits across the lines.
- The large billboard is behind the vehicle on the -Z side, a little toward +X, so it stands right of the vehicle in the opening frame of the master. The opening track of the master runs from 15 degrees off the nose to 26 degrees off it, so the vehicle is seen nearly head on at 0 s and as a front three-quarter by 7.5 s, and the background and the near deck move against it during the push. In the vertical format the track runs from 40 to 26 degrees off the nose and further back, so one tall tower stands over the vehicle, and the billboard is outside the frame to the right. A wheel stop and a drain grate lie on the near deck inside the opening frame of both formats. Nothing on the near deck is cut by a corner of the frame at the judged frames. The billboard reflects along the upper body surfaces when the camera looks toward the rear.
- The single red sign is on the same side as the billboard. The stair core is on the +X side near the -Z parapet.
- Focal lengths come only from `lens.focalLengthsMm`. A focal length gives the field of view across the long side of a 36 mm gate. In `formats.master` the long side is horizontal. In `formats.vertical` the camera is turned on its side, so the long side is vertical.
- The lens is anamorphic in character: oval bokeh and a restrained streak. The squeeze does not change the field of view figures above.
- Stop: T2 on the wide shots, T4 on the wheel and lamp shots, T11 on the detail shot so the whole grille holds, and T11 on the paint shot: the image of the billboard in the clearcoat is a virtual image hundreds of meters behind the panel, and at T4 its blur circle is 9 percent of the frame width, so the sign dissolves into a wash. At T11 the edge of the image is soft by about 3 percent of the frame width, as in the key frame, and the depth of field on the panel is still only 3.5 centimeters. The aperture is sampled, so depth of field follows from these.
- Camera positions name the lens entrance pupil. Heights are above the deck.
- Rig limits come from `reference/board/13-camera-moves-on-vehicles.md`: slider travel up to 1 m at up to 0.13 m per second, dolly up to 3 m of track at 0.2 to 0.6 m per second, crane and jib arm starts and stops eased over at least 1 second, pan and tilt 2 to 15 degrees per second. The `wheel` shot is on a small jib arm, not a slider, so the slider speed limit does not apply to it. The `lamp` shot is on a short dolly track, so the dolly limits apply to it.
- Easing: a smooth S curve. Each end of a move takes at least 15 percent of the move, and never less than 1 second on the dolly and the crane. The dolly and the crane keep each end to 25 percent or less. The sliders and the jib may take longer over a start that follows a cut, so the move is not at speed while the eye is still finding the frame.
- Operator motion sits on every shot: seeded, periodic over the film, at most 0.12 degrees of rotation and 2 mm of travel on the wide shots, a quarter of that on the macro shots. Frequencies 0.2 to 1.5 Hz. It never reads as shake.
- Cuts happen only at beat boundaries. `arrival` into `wake` is one continuous move with no cut. `return` begins with a cut to a raised three-quarter view and ends on the opening frame, so the loop has no cut.
- Every insert is on an 85 or 100 mm lens, 0.3 to 3.8 m from its subject, and the subject fills the frame. One insert holds a whole part: the master `wheel` shot shows the whole wheel under its arch, standing on the deck, because no tighter 16:9 frame clears the rim, the tire crown and the arch lip without a border lying along one of them. The vertical `wheel` shot is the macro of the same wheel. Each insert has its own height, direction and move so no two read as the same setup: `paint` looks down across the wing and travels along it with the vent slot in frame, `wheel` arcs round the wheel from above the wing line on the jib in the master and booms from arch to deck in the vertical, `lamp` pushes from the whole signature into one projector lens, `detail` looks aft and down into the open engine bay from over the trailing edge of the roof, with the whole grille, its bezel, the carbon brace and the cam cover in frame in the master, and the cells with the edge of the cam cover in the vertical.
- Rain on the front element: none in this cut.
- The `detail` anchor is the left-hand airbox grille in the open engine bay under the rear deck: a hexagonal mesh panel 0.42 m long and 0.18 m wide with a plain margin, a flush duct behind the mesh and the stepped carbon side panel beside it. The near cam cover and the carbon brace stand beside it in the view from over the roof, and the painted ledge and shoulder lie outboard of it.
