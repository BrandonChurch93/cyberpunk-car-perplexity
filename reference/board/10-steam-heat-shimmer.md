## Steam and heat shimmer in rain

What to learn: Shape, density, how light passes through

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/10-steam-heat-shimmer/` (`s1.jpg` to `s9.jpg`).

### Sources

#### S1 · Drain steam rising over rain-wet asphalt under night city lights (1/50 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Subway_steam_in_NYC.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Subway_steam_in_NYC.jpg
- Shows: Street drain steam rising beside a parked vehicle on rain-slicked asphalt at night at 1/50 s, f/1.5 under headlamps, an amber streetlamp, and a pink LED billboard (cache s1.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Low-velocity drain steam hugs the wet asphalt for 0.3 to 0.5 m laterally around the front left tyre before buoyancy lifts it vertically past bumper and hood height.
  2. Where the foreground left headlamp shines forward through the low steam wisps (0.4 to 0.8 m off the deck), strong forward scattering drives steam luminance to 85 to 98 percent white, roughly 4 to 6 times brighter than adjacent unlit wisps outside the beam.
  3. Three urban emitters tint separate pockets of the same rising column without blending into uniform grey: rear blue-white headlamps dye the 1.5 to 2.5 m zone cool cyan-blue, a left streetlamp dyes the 3 to 5 m core warm amber-gold, and the vertical pink LED sign on the right dyes upper-right wisps pale magenta.
  4. Between 6 and 12 m height, turbulent air entrainment feathers the plume into ragged wisps whose opacity fades smoothly to zero; lit office windows behind the thinning upper steam remain geometrically straight while losing 40 to 70 percent contrast.

#### S2 · Wind-bent steam vent stack in front of bright LED billboards (1/25 s, 18 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Steam_near_Times_Square,_New_York_City.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Steam_near_Times_Square%2C_New_York_City.jpg
- Shows: Striped ~300 mm street steam pipe venting across bright night LED billboards at 1/25 s, f/8.0, 18 mm (cache s2.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Right at the top rim of the ~300 mm vent pipe, hot vapor exits nearly transparent for the first 5 to 12 cm (0.2 to 0.4 pipe diameters) before mixing with cold night air triggers dense white condensation.
  2. Crosswind bends the momentum jet to 55 to 60 degrees from vertical while turbulent entrainment widens the plume linearly from ~0.3 m at the rim to ~3.5 m across over ~6 m of travel (14 to 16 degree half-angle).
  3. Wind shear along the upper boundary rolls the steam into repeating Kelvin-Helmholtz billow lobes spaced 0.3 to 0.6 m apart, while the lower leeward edge trails softer wisps.
  4. Bright LED billboards directly behind the plume show two distinct optical-depth regimes: through the thick core (optical depth above 2.5) dark facade detail is veiled in uniform pink-grey scattered light, while through the mid-density outer zone (optical depth 0.6 to 1.8) the vertical white sign punches a bright forward-scattered halo 3 to 5 times brighter than the front-lit pipe end and blurs letter edges across 15 to 25 cm.

#### S3 · Open street manhole plume lit from below, behind, and above (1/30 s, 18 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22Gate_to_hell%22_(7185248460).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bb/%22Gate_to_hell%22_%287185248460%29.jpg
- Shows: Condensed steam rising from an open ~600 mm street manhole at dusk at 1/30 s, f/3.5, 18 mm with internal pit lighting, rear red brake lamps, and an overhead green traffic signal (cache s3.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. With low exit momentum, the ~0.6 m wide plume rises nearly straight up for 0.8 m before drifting with the street breeze and expanding to ~2.5 m width at 4 m elevation.
  2. Warm work lights inside the pit illuminate the dense base from underneath in bright amber-gold, which extinguishes rapidly within 1.0 to 1.2 m vertically because multiple scattering traps upward-directed light near the dense mouth.
  3. At 1.5 to 2.0 m height, red brake lamps behind the plume transmit through moderate steam density as soft cherry-red glowing balls 4 to 6 times wider than the lamp lenses, hiding crisp lamp edges while preserving the dark vehicle silhouette.
  4. At 3.5 to 4.5 m height where the plume thins below 35 percent opacity, an overhead green traffic light shines cleanly through the wisps and tints a ~0.8 m pocket emerald-cyan while facade windows behind stay legible.

#### S4 · Close-up of buoyant steam tongues splitting at a cast-iron drain rim (1/13 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:(2005)_NYC%27s_Steamy_Manhole_(5684925415).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/%282005%29_NYC%27s_Steamy_Manhole_%285684925415%29.jpg
- Shows: Close view at 1/13 s, f/5.0 of low-pressure steam escaping a ~600 mm cast-iron manhole frame on dark asphalt (cache s4.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Low-velocity drain vapor does not rise as a solid cylinder; buoyant instability splits it right at the grate rim into 4 to 6 separate twisting tongues and ribbons 6 to 15 cm wide.
  2. At 1/13 s (77 ms), upward buoyant motion (~0.6 to 1.2 m/s) blurs small turbulent curls into soft fibrous streaks 5 to 10 cm long tilted ~25 degrees by light wind.
  3. Opacity peaks at 60 to 85 percent in the bottom 15 to 40 cm above the rim and drops below 25 percent within 1.0 m height as drier ambient air mixes inward.
  4. Clear gaps between individual steam tongues expose dark asphalt and painted lane lines right down to the rim, giving a 30 to 50 percent void fraction near the grate.

#### S5 · Ground-hugging steam sheet creeping across asphalt before lift-off (1/40 s, 35 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Steam_in_new_york_street_(Unsplash).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Steam_in_new_york_street_%28Unsplash%29.jpg
- Shows: Street-level steam spreading across asphalt in a breeze at 1/40 s, f/3.2, 35 mm with a second elevated vent plume rising past a storefront awning (cache s5.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Under street wind shear, low-momentum drain vapor creeps horizontally for 3 to 4 m as a shallow 20 to 60 cm thick carpet before buoyant eddies detach upward.
  2. Wind gusts break the creeping sheet into rounded cauliflower puffs 0.3 to 0.8 m wide separated by clear lanes of bare pavement.
  3. The upper plume demonstrates background-dependent contrast sign flip: it reads bright white (> 80 percent luminance) in front of the dark shop interior, then shifts to a slightly darker warm-grey silhouette where it crosses bright sunlit stone above because out-scattering exceeds local in-scattering.
  4. Behind the low puffs, high-frequency wheel and leg edges soften first while broad light-dark shapes remain intact.

#### S6 · Compact rooftop-scale metal hood vent backlit at night (1/15 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Halal_Steam_(100012809).jpeg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Halal_Steam_%28100012809%29.jpeg
- Shows: Warm moist exhaust rising at night from a ~2 m high metal roof vent above bright fluorescent hood lamps against a dark tower facade at 1/15 s, f/4.5 (cache s6.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Moist exhaust from a small unpressurized roof vent forms a wispy, semi-transparent plume tilted ~35 degrees by night breeze, never exceeding ~30 to 45 percent opacity outside the immediate lip.
  2. Within 0.5 m of the bright white hood fixture, forward and side scattering lights the vapor to 60 to 85 percent luminance.
  3. Between 1.0 and 3.0 m above the roof lip, where the plume enters unlit air in front of the dark facade, brightness drops rapidly by inverse-square lamp falloff to 5 to 12 percent luminance before vanishing by 3.5 m height.
  4. Vertical dark facade mullions behind the wispy plume stay straight without measurable refractive wobble, showing that cool diluted steam scatters light without visibly warping geometry.

#### S7 · Pure exhaust heat shimmer warping structural steel beams (1/320 s, 50 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Asuka3_20250925_104324.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/Asuka3_20250925_104324.jpg
- Shows: Hot uncondensed exhaust rising from twin cylindrical stacks (~0.6 to 0.8 m wide) in front of a steel truss bridge at 1/320 s, f/7.1, 50 mm (cache s7.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Dry superheated exhaust is 100 percent transparent with zero white fog, zero extinction darkening, and zero color tint; it is visible solely by refractive displacement of background edges.
  2. Straight steel truss diagonals behind the hot plumes bend into wavy ripples with 5 to 20 px peak-to-peak lateral offset (on a 1280 px wide image, ~1 to 3 arcminutes angular deflection) at eddy scales of 5 to 15 cm.
  3. Refractive warping peaks between 0.2 and 2.5 stack diameters (0.15 to 2.0 m) above the metal lip and fades below 1 to 2 px beyond 4 stack diameters as ambient air entrainment flattens temperature gradients.
  4. Just 0.3 m outside the conical plume boundary, steel flanges and handrail posts remain completely straight and sharp.

#### S9 · Grazing-angle thermal boundary shimmer over heated asphalt (1/400 s, 128 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_Highway_Mirage_(5994891327).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/A_Highway_Mirage_%285994891327%29.jpg
- Shows: Shallow grazing view (< 1 degree elevation) along heated asphalt road showing inferior mirage reflections and low thermal shimmer at 1/400 s, f/7.1, 128 mm (cache s9.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Continuous upward ray bending inside the thin 0.5 to 2 cm air thermal boundary layer produces flat mirror patches reflecting bright sky and distant car bodies at viewing angles under ~0.5 to 1.0 degrees.
  2. Rising buoyant thermal sheets in the lowest 10 to 30 cm vertically stretch and oscillate distant tyres and tail-lamps.
  3. On foreground pavement closer than ~40 m where camera grazing angle exceeds ~1.5 degrees, ray deflection (~0.03 to 0.1 degrees) is too weak to produce visible shimmer or mirage.
  4. In steady rain, cold precipitation (`4 to 9 m/s` fall speed) rapidly cools open asphalt slabs, eliminating wide road-surface heat shimmer and confining real shimmer in rain to hot localized sources such as vehicle tailpipes/engine bays and rooftop HVAC/boiler exhaust flues.

### Real-world sizes
- Condensed steam droplet diameter vs raindrop diameter: condensing vapor jets (`T_s = 106 to 172 C` mixing into cooler air) nucleate liquid water micro-droplets `0.5 to 22 um` in diameter (droplets under `50 um` follow turbulent Gaussian air dispersion with negligible fall speed), roughly 100 to 500 times smaller than `1.0 to 5.0 mm` (`1000 to 5000 um`) raindrops falling ballistically at `4.03 to 9.09 m/s`. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14787 and https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0736/ML073620431.pdf and https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Steam jet-to-plume hydrodynamics and entrainment: near a pressurized vent of radius `R_s`, flow starts as a momentum-dominated jet scaling with `R_s` (typical industrial/plant stack exit speeds `1.0 to 10.9 m/s`, low-pressure drain vents `0.3 to 1.2 m/s`) before transitioning at crossover height `z_c` to a buoyancy-dominated plume whose width and height scale with mass flux as `m_dot^(2/5)` and whose horizontal ambient-air entrainment velocity equals `0.3` times local vertical rise speed (giving a ~14 to 16 degree half-angle cone in calm air, matching S2). Cool, high-humidity rain air (`RH > 90 percent`) slows droplet re-evaporation and extends visible steam plume length by 2 to 4 times compared with dry air. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14787 and https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0736/ML073620431.pdf)
- Air refractive index temperature gradient for heat shimmer (Ciddor equation at `550 nm`): `n(550 nm, T) = 1 + 2.778e-4 * (288.15 K / T)`, yielding `dn / dT = -0.96e-6 K^-1` near `15 C` (about `1.0e-5` refractive index drop per `10 K` air heating, or `~1.0e-4` for a `100 K` hot exhaust plume). Over warm horizontal surfaces, air temperature decays exponentially as `T(y) = T_cool + Delta_T * exp(-y / beta)` with thermal boundary layer thickness `beta = 0.3 to 2.0 cm` over pavement and `5.0 cm` over warm water. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09131v1)
- Reference raindrop, streak, splash, and wave scales interacting with steam on the deck: raindrops `1.0 to 5.0 mm` (`4.03 to 9.09 m/s`) trace `67 to 152 mm` streaks at `1/60 s` shutter; hard-surface splash crowns span `16 to 40 mm` width and `6 to 22 mm` height over a `15 to 35 ms` lifetime; puddle rings propagate at `0.14 to 0.44 m/s` (`sqrt(g * h)` for `2 to 20 mm` depths, bounded by the `0.23 m/s` capillary-gravity minimum). (sources: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf and https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17098v2)

### Build notes
- Deck drain grate steam (`low momentum, v_exit = 0.4 to 0.9 m/s`): emit 4 to 6 separate clumpy ribbons (`6 to 15 cm` initial width, 30 to 50 percent clear gaps at the grate rim, S4) that creep `0.3 to 0.8 m` horizontally along the wet deck with the wind (`0.2 to 0.4 m` layer height, S1, S5) before lifting at `25 to 40 degrees` and fading completely by `1.8 to 3.0 m` height.
- Rooftop plant and HVAC flue steam (`moderate momentum, v_exit = 1.5 to 3.5 m/s` from a `0.25 to 0.40 m` vent): leave a `5 to 10 cm` semi-transparent condensation gap right at the hot metal lip (S2), bend the jet `45 to 60 degrees` into the shared wind direction (same wind vector that tilts the rain `8 to 16 degrees`), expand cone width at a `15 degree` half-angle (`0.3x` entrainment ratio) with `0.3 to 0.6 m` Kelvin-Helmholtz upper-edge billows, and fade optical depth smoothly to zero over `3.5 to 6.0 m` travel (S2, S6).
- Forward-peaked Mie scattering in steam (`0.5 to 22 um` droplets, `Henyey-Greenstein g = 0.65 to 0.80`): when a vehicle lamp, luminaire, or bright neon/LED sign sits behind the steam within a `20 to 35 degree` forward cone, boost scattered radiance 3 to 5 times over side-lit steam and tint that local pocket with the emitter's exact color while radially blurring sharp background edges behind optical depths `0.5 to 2.0` (S1, S2, S3).
- Beer-Lambert extinction and dark-zone falloff: more than `3 m` away from lights in front of dark sky or unlit facades, drop steam luminance below `8 to 12 percent` so unlit vapor never self-glows at night (S6), and let dense foreground wisps (`optical depth > 1.0`) crossing directly in front of bright signage slightly dim the sign face by out-scattering extinction (S5).
- Keep heat shimmer dry, subtle, and strictly localized in rain: do not apply road-mirage shimmer across the rain-cooled wet deck; place a small 3D transparent volume only over active hot exhausts (vehicle rear exhaust outlets if warm, and the first `0.15 to 1.5 m` above rooftop boiler or plant hot flues) with zero opacity/fog and a screen-space UV refraction offset of `1 to 4 px` at `1080p` driven by `5 to 15 cm` upward-advecting curl-noise eddies at `1.5 to 3.0 m/s` rise speed, partially motion-softened for the `1/60 s` shutter (S7, S8, S9).
