## Rain lit from behind

What to learn: Streak length and brightness against exposure, density with distance, how it vanishes over dark areas

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/04-rain-lit-from-behind/` (`s1.jpg` to `s9.jpg`).

#### S2 · Oncoming car headlamps lighting wind-tilted streaks (1/30 s, 50 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cars_light_up_the_rain.JPG
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/49/Cars_light_up_the_rain.JPG
- Shows: Close oncoming vehicle headlamps backlighting slanted rain streaks on a dark tree-lined road at 1/30 s, f/1.8, 50 mm (cache s2.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At 1/30 s exposure (33.3 ms), mid-ground raindrops 4 to 8 m in front of the headlamps trace parallel streaks spanning 5 to 11 percent of frame height, all tilted 16 to 20 degrees from vertical by crosswind.
  2. Drops passing 1 to 2 m from the 50 mm f/1.8 lens defocus into soft translucent ribbons 8 to 16 px wide (on a 1280 px wide image) with feathered edges and roughly one-third to one-half the peak contrast of focused mid-ground streaks.
  3. Streak luminance peaks inside a 15 to 20 degree forward-scattering lobe around each headlamp axis at 80 to 95 percent luminance, then falls by more than three-quarters beyond two car widths off-axis.
  4. Over the unlit upper canopy where no light source sits behind the falling drops, rain streaks vanish to black.

#### S3 · Elevated view comparing oncoming and receding headlamp cones (1/100 s, 50 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Farol_ilumina_a_chuva_de_noite.JPG

- Shows: Night street viewed from above at 1/100 s, f/1.8, 50 mm with one car approaching the camera and another facing away (cache s3.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Shortening exposure from 1/30 s (S2) to 1/100 s (10 ms) at the same 50 mm focal length cuts mid-ground streak length to about 1.5 to 3.2 percent of frame height while raising individual streak sharpness and contrast.

  3. Streaks crossing within 0.5 m of the approaching headlamp cores merge into bright white wedges, whereas streaks 2 to 3 m laterally off-axis separate into crisp 1 to 2 px hairlines against dark wet asphalt.
  4. In the shadowed lower-right road surface and upper-left hillside outside the headlamp cones, rain vanishes completely.

#### S5 · Slow-shutter oncoming beam and wet street reflections (1/20 s, 50 mm)

- Shows: Rainy night intersection at 1/20 s, f/1.8, 50 mm with an oncoming car lighting a dense rain curtain in the midground (cache s5.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At 1/20 s (50 ms), rain streaks inside the oncoming headlamp zone stretch to 7 to 15 percent of frame height, tilted 6 to 8 degrees from vertical.
  2. Longer shutter integration lowers the contrast of smaller drops and blends mid-to-far rain inside the beam core into a milky forward-scattered haze, leaving only large 2 to 4 mm drops readable as separate streaks.
  3. Foreground wet pavement outside the direct headlamp cone stays dark at 3 to 6 percent luminance with no airborne streaks visible over it, showing only elongated specular reflections of distant lights.

#### S6 · Commuter rail platform with amber LED matrix sign and pole lights (1/80 s, 16 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MBTA_commuter_rail_train_at_Boston_Landing_in_the_rain.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0d/MBTA_commuter_rail_train_at_Boston_Landing_in_the_rain.jpg
- Shows: Rain at night on an outdoor rail platform at 1/80 s, f/4.0, 16 mm backlit by an orange LED message board and white platform poles (cache s6.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At 1/80 s (12.5 ms) on a 16 mm wide lens, depth separates rain into three clear scales: foreground drops 1.5 to 4 m away span 12 to 30 px, mid-platform drops 15 to 25 m away shrink to 3 to 7 px grain, and lamps beyond 50 m show smooth halos with no resolved streaks.
  2. Rain streaks passing within 1.0 to 1.5 m above, across, and below the orange LED sign pick up saturated amber-orange color, while streaks lit by the white pole fixtures behind them stay neutral white.
  3. Streaks are hardest to see directly on top of saturated white lamp cores where background luminance is already clipped; they pop most strongly in the dark collar 0.1 to 1.5 m outside the emitter silhouette where background luminance drops sharply.
  4. In the unlit upper-left sky quadrant away from platform fixtures, rain streaks vanish completely into black sky.

#### S9 · Five-second exposure across a rain-soaked night parking lot (5.0 s, 24 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gillette_Stadium_at_night_in_the_rain_November_2025.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Gillette_Stadium_at_night_in_the_rain_November_2025.jpg
- Shows: Tripod 5.0 s exposure at f/8.0, 24 mm over a wet parking lot in steady night rain (cache s9.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At a 5.0 s shutter duration, airborne raindrop dwell time per pixel (under 1.2 ms) is less than 1/4000 of the exposure, so individual falling rain streaks integrate to zero visible contrast across the entire image.
  2. Airborne rain contributes only smooth radial scattering halos around the parking lot luminaires and an even warm-grey bounce on the low cloud deck, confirming inverse brightness scaling with shutter open time.
  3. Stationary droplets sitting on the front lens glass remain visible as soft circular bokeh patches because they occupy the same sensor pixels for the entire 5.0 s exposure.
  4. Wet asphalt bay markings remain crisp horizontally while luminaire reflections stretch vertically across the foreground deck.

### Real-world sizes
- Raindrop diameter range before aerodynamic breakup: 0.5 mm (small drizzle) to 5.0 to 5.8 mm maximum stable diameter; drops below 2.0 mm diameter stay nearly spherical while 2.0 to 5.0 mm drops flatten on the bottom into oblate hamburger buns. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Median drop diameter, fall speed, and flux by rain rate: drizzle (0.025 cm/h) has 0.96 mm median diameter, 4.1 m/s speed, 151 drops/m2/s; light rain (0.10 cm/h) has 1.24 mm, 4.8 m/s, 280 drops/m2/s; moderate rain (0.38 cm/h) has 1.60 mm, 5.7 m/s, 495 drops/m2/s; heavy rain (1.52 cm/h) has 2.05 mm, 6.7 m/s, 495 drops/m2/s; excessive rain (4.06 cm/h) has 2.40 mm, 7.3 m/s, 818 drops/m2/s; cloudburst (10.2 cm/h) has 2.85 mm, 7.9 m/s, 1220 drops/m2/s. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Sea-level terminal velocity by exact drop diameter (Gunn and Kinzer 1949, 1013 mb, 20 C): 0.5 mm falls at 2.06 m/s; 1.0 mm at 4.03 m/s; 1.6 mm at 5.65 m/s; 2.0 mm at 6.49 m/s; 2.6 mm at 7.57 m/s; 3.0 mm at 8.06 m/s; 4.0 mm at 8.83 m/s; 5.0 mm at 9.09 m/s; 5.8 mm at 9.17 m/s. (source: https://www.madsci.org/posts/archives/2000-07/962626446.Ph.r.html and https://www.atmos.albany.edu/facstaff/rfovell/ATM562/foote-dutoit-1969.pdf)
- World-space streak length against shutter time `L = v * T`: at the film configuration of 30 fps and 180 degree shutter (`T = 1/60 s = 16.67 ms`), a 1.0 mm drop (`4.03 m/s`) makes a 67 mm streak, a 1.6 mm moderate-rain drop (`5.65 m/s`) makes a 94 mm streak, a 2.0 mm heavy-rain drop (`6.49 m/s`) makes a 108 mm streak, a 3.0 mm drop (`8.06 m/s`) makes a 134 mm streak, and a 5.0 mm drop (`9.09 m/s`) makes a 152 mm streak. Projected image length is `L_px = (f_px * v * T) / z`. (source: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf)
- Pixel dwell time and streak brightness vs exposure (Garg and Nayar 2007): a falling drop crosses a point in world space in dwell time `tau = 2a / v` (with upper bound `4a / v = sqrt(a / 50) s`, always under 1.18 ms even for a 3.5 mm radius drop). Added pixel intensity over background is `Delta_I = k0 * (tau / T) * (L_r - L_b)` for near drops and `Delta_I = k0 * (4 * f_px * a^2 / (z * v * T)) * (L_r - L_b)` for far drops, so streak brightness falls as `1 / T` with exposure time and as `1 / z` with distance once streak width drops below one pixel, while lens defocus reduces streak contrast by `w / (w + b_c)` where `b_c` is the blur circle diameter. (source: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf)

### Build notes
- Lock world-space streak length to `L = v(d) * T_shutter` evaluated once at frame center time (no temporal jitter double-blurring): with `fps = 30` and `shutterDegrees = 180` (`T_shutter = 0.01667 s`), draw streaks 0.067 m long for 1.0 mm drops, 0.108 m for 2.0 mm drops, and 0.135 to 0.150 m for 3.0 to 5.0 mm drops, tilted 8 to 16 degrees from vertical along a shared wind vector with +/- 2 degrees per-drop flutter.
- Couple streak opacity strictly to local backlighting and beam intersection: raindrops refract strongly forward through a 15 to 25 degree cone (`(L_r - L_b)` term) and reflect a secondary rim glint up to ~42 degrees; over unlit sky, dark facades, and shadowed asphalt outside luminaire, headlamp, tail-lamp, or neon/LED cones, drive streak alpha below 0.01 so streaks disappear over dark areas as seen in S1, S2, S3, S4, and S6.
- Tint streaks by the local light volume they traverse: streaks within 1.5 to 3.0 m of colored neon or LED signage inherit that sign's RGB color (S4, S6), streaks inside pole luminaire cones inherit the lamp spectrum (S1, S4), and streaks inside vehicle headlamp or tail-lamp cones inherit white or red forward scatter (S2, S3).
- Near lens layer (`0.8 to 3.5 m` from camera): render sparse discrete streaks (`1.0 to 3.5 mm` diameter), widened by circle-of-confusion `b_c` and attenuated in peak alpha by `w / (w + b_c)` so close out-of-focus streaks read as soft translucent ribbons 6 to 18 px wide (with 2x horizontal anamorphic defocus oval stretch), never opaque white bars (S2, S8).
- Mid-deck layer (`3.5 to 22 m` from camera): render the main geometric streak field using a realistic drop-size mix (mostly 1.2 to 2.6 mm diameters, `90 to 130 mm` world streak lengths), modulated along each streak by subtle 15 to 30 percent intensity beading on large drops (S8) and grouped by low-frequency 3D wind-gust noise (`2 to 4 m` spatial wavelength, S7) so rain falls in moving sheets.
- Far background layer (`beyond 22 to 25 m`): stop drawing individual streak geometry once projected width `2a * f_px / z` is sub-pixel (`1/z` contrast regime); fold distant rain into the volumetric ray-marched light-cone scattering and distant skyline extinction haze (S1, S6, S7).
