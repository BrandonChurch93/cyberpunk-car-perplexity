## Real camera moves on vehicles

What to learn: Speed, easing and framing of dolly, slider and crane shots

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/camera-moves-on-vehicles/` (c01 to c06; c02, c04 and c06 viewed as rig reference only).

Note on numbers: most equipment sheets give top speeds and travel, not easing curves. Where a speed or time below is worked out from a stated length and duration, it says "derived".

### Sources

#### S1 · SuperTechno 48 telescopic crane, maker spec sheet
- URL: https://www.supertechno.com/download.php?fid=2073
- Image: none used
- Shows: Technocrane's own specification sheet for its large five beam telescopic crane.
- Observations:
  1. Maximum telescopic travel is 39 ft 4 in (about 12.0 m), maximum length from center 48 ft 3 in, and maximum lens height 51 ft 10 in overslung.
  2. Telescope speed is stated as 2 m per second (6.56 ft per second). A full 12 m extension therefore takes at least 6 s (derived).
  3. The sheet stresses minimal deflection during "rapid starts and stops", which is the limit on how hard a move can ease in and out.
  4. Column elevation travel is 3 ft 2 in, so most of the height change comes from arm tilt, not the post.

#### S2 · Technocrane 22, rental house spec
- URL: https://www.procamrentals.com/technocrane-22
- Image: none used
- Shows: Specification of the common 22 ft Technocrane used on commercials.
- Observations:
  1. Telescopic range is 15 ft 6 in (4.73 m) at up to 4 ft 9 in per second (1.45 m per second). Full travel takes at least about 3.3 s (derived).
  2. Maximum lens height is 6.62 m underslung and 7.32 m overslung.
  3. The arm runs from 1.5 m to 6.22 m post to nose, so the camera can push in or pull out about 4.7 m without moving the base.
  4. The chassis is only 1.09 by 0.79 m, small enough to sit beside a parked car.

#### S3 · Technodolly 25, maker price list with specs
- URL: https://www.supertechno.com/download.php?fid=2136
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Technodolly_15.jpg/1280px-Technodolly_15.jpg (the smaller Technodolly 15 on track, from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Technodolly_15.jpg, cache c06)
- Shows: The programmable telescopic crane on a motorized base used for repeatable moves.
- Observations:
  1. Maximum lens height 7.24 m and maximum telescopic travel 5.75 m.
  2. Telescopic arm speed is adjustable from 0 to 1.0 m per second; 5.75 m takes at least 5.75 s (derived).
  3. The sheet lists "adjustable fluidity of telescopic start and stop", meaning the ease in and ease out is a set parameter, not a fixed curve. No seconds are given.
  4. The photo of the Technodolly 15 shows the crane base sitting on straight track with the arm and remote head above, the layout used for repeatable passes along a car.

#### S4 · MovieBird 45 telescopic crane
- URL: https://www.moviecrane.gr/equipment/moviebird-45/
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/MovieBird_in_production.jpg/1280px-MovieBird_in_production.jpg (from https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MovieBird_in_production.jpg, cache c02)
- Shows: Spec page for a 13.7 m telescopic crane, plus a photo of a MovieBird working on location.
- Observations:
  1. Telescoping length 13.7 m, maximum lens height 11.10 m, minimum reach 2.60 m.
  2. Maximum telescopic speed is 1.5 m per second.
  3. The crane must not be used in wind faster than 5.5 to 7 m per second, so big crane moves are slow and steady in practice.
  4. In the photo the arm reaches far out from a truck mounted base with the remote head hanging at the nose, the base well outside the frame.

#### S5 · MRMC Bolt high speed camera robot
- URL: https://www.mrmoco.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2025/04/BOLT-Leaflet.pdf
- Image: none used
- Shows: Maker leaflet for the robot arm used for fast car commercial moves and repeat passes.
- Observations:
  1. Maximum speed at camera: 7 m per second from the arm, 5 m per second on track, 12 m per second arm and track together.
  2. "Practical shooting speeds": 1 m vertical or horizontal in 0.5 s. That is 2 m per second average; with an even ramp up and ramp down it means about 4 m per second peak and about 16 m per second squared acceleration (derived).
  3. A full 180° arc of 0.75 m radius takes 1.5 s, about 1.6 m per second along the path (derived); a 180° arc of 0.45 m radius takes 1 s.
  4. Maximum lens height 3.5 m, lowest position 0 m, reach 2.0 m from rotate center: the arm covers ground level to above a car roof.

#### S6 · U-Crane (Russian Arm) on a chase car
- URL: https://www.southeastarmcar.com/u-crane-arm-filmotechnic-la-miami-dallas-fort-worth-texas-orlando-detroit-new-york-new-mexico-a
- Image: none used
- Shows: Spec list for the gyro stabilized crane arm on a camera car used to shoot moving cars.
- Observations:
  1. Reach from the center post is 14 ft (4.27 m), extendable to 19 ft; the fulcrum sits 8 ft off the ground.
  2. Minimum safe camera height off the road is 12 in (0.30 m); maximum lens height 14 ft.
  3. Pan speed is 360° in 6 s (60° per second). At 4.27 m reach the camera tip travels about 4.5 m per second when swinging at full rate (derived).
  4. "With an 18 mm lens a full frame top shot can be achieved", and the system runs at vehicle speeds up to 130 mph (58 m per second).

#### S7 · U-Crane Dynamic, compact arm
- URL: https://www.precisiondrivers.de/u-crane/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Spec list for the smaller arm on a Porsche Macan used for race tracks and tight streets.
- Observations:
  1. Arm length 3.43 m, tilt 45°, pan 360°.
  2. Pan speed max 360° in 6.5 s, about 55° per second; tip speed about 3.3 m per second at full reach (derived).
  3. Tilt speed max 45° in 1.5 s, 30° per second; at 3.43 m that lifts or drops the camera about 2.4 m in 1.5 s (derived from arm length and tilt angle).
  4. Payload 24 kg and vehicle speed up to 200 km/h.

#### S9 · Crane camera on a night police car scene, Transformers
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Filming_of_Transformers_at_Edwards_AFB_2007-02-28_2.jpg
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Filming_of_Transformers_at_Edwards_AFB_2007-02-28_2.jpg/1280px-Filming_of_Transformers_at_Edwards_AFB_2007-02-28_2.jpg
- Shows: A crane-mounted remote head camera beside a police car on an airfield apron at night. (cache c05)
- Observations:
  1. The camera head, wrapped in clear plastic against weather, hangs at about the car roof height, roughly 1.4 to 1.6 m.
  2. The crane base sits only a couple of meters from the car, so even a crane shot here is close and wide rather than high and long.
  3. Blue and red light bars on the car are the strongest light sources and throw color onto the wet looking concrete and the aircraft above.
  4. The car is placed under the wing so the aircraft skin forms a reflective ceiling, catching blue light from the light bar.

#### S10 · Chapman Hybrid IV camera dolly
- URL: https://thatcat.com/product/hybrid-iv-dolly/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Specs of the standard hydraulic arm dolly used on track for car beauty passes.
- Observations:
  1. Vertical boom travel is 1.09 m (3 ft 7 in).
  2. Camera mount height runs from 48 cm to 1.57 m without risers, 1.88 m with a 12 in riser, and down to 3 cm with a low 90° plate: low car shots near the ground are a standard setup.
  3. Payload up to 318 kg on the arm.
  4. The maker lists a float mode for continuous up and down arm moves and a heater "for constant arm speed": smooth boom speed is a design goal, but no speed figure is published.

#### S12 · Rhino motorized slider
- URL: https://www.filmtools.com/rhino-camera-gear-ultimate-slider-bundle-with-soft-shell-case-camera-tripod-base.html
- Image: none used
- Shows: Spec table for a common 42 in motorized slider.
- Observations:
  1. Track length 42 in (1.07 m).
  2. High speed motor top speed 5 in per second (0.127 m per second), so a full 42 in slide takes at least about 8.4 s (derived).
  3. High torque motor for vertical moves: 1 in per second (0.025 m per second).
  4. The Arc II head on the same kit pans at 19° per second max and tilts at 7.5° per second max: slider moves are slow, small parallax moves.

#### S13 · DJI Ronin 2 gimbal
- URL: https://www.dji.com/ronin-2/info
- Image: none used
- Shows: Maker spec for a three axis gimbal often hung on cranes and car mounts.
- Observations:
  1. Maximum controlled rotation speed: pan 400° per second (the FAQ says 360° per second), tilt 360° per second, roll 360° per second.
  2. Payload 13.6 kg.
  3. The gimbal sets rotation only; linear speed comes from whatever carries it.
  4. Its rotation limits are far above what a smooth car shot uses, so pan speed in practice is set by the operator, not the gimbal.

#### S14 · A oner through car wrecks on a Technocrane 22
- URL: https://mattglen.com/pages/shooting-the-oner-with-a-technocrane
- Image: none used
- Shows: A cinematographer's write-up of a night road safety commercial shot as one continuous crane move through cars.
- Observations:
  1. Camera was a small mirrorless body with a 24 mm f/1.4 at ISO 12800, on a Ronin RS2 gimbal hung from a Technocrane 22.
  2. Light came mainly from practical headlights and emergency lights, because there was no budget for a large overhead source.
  3. The crane base was machined down so the arm could slide through both side windows and a broken windscreen; the move needed 20 plus takes to get it through at the right speed.
  4. Timing was planned in a rough 3D animatic first, with 3 hidden cuts; the whole shoot had 5 hours at night.

#### S15 · Ford v Ferrari, lenses and distance on race cars
- URL: https://www.panavision.com/highlights/highlights-detail/ford-v-ferrari-delivers-speed-and-danger-with-classic-approach
- Image: none used
- Shows: Panavision article on how Phedon Papamichael shot the racing.
- Observations:
  1. The cinematographer said he "would have been happy to shoot everything on the 30, the 35, and the 40mm" on the large format sensor.

#### S16 · Spectre, ASC on the Rome car chase
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/sinister-sect-spectre/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Hoyte van Hoytema on camera movement in the night chase.
- Observations:
  1. The goal was frames "more elegant and settled", conveying speed without shaking the image.
  2. Cameras were mounted to the cars and a Russian Arm was used a lot.
  3. Lenses were Arri Master Anamorphics at T1.9.
  4. Car interiors were shot on a stage with rear-screen projection, LED panels, rotating mirrors and moving light panels, so the camera did not have to travel there at all.

### Build notes
- Slider: travel 0.3 to 1.0 m, speed 0.02 to 0.13 m/s, move length 6 to 15 s. Ease in and out over the first and last 15 to 25 percent of the move. Lens 35 to 85 mm equivalent; camera 0.3 to 1.2 m from the car for detail (badges, beads, light edge).
- Dolly: travel 1.8 to 3 m on short track, speed about 0.2 to 0.6 m/s, move length 4 to 12 s (derived ranges from track length and a slow push, not published speeds). Boom range up to 1.09 m during the move. Camera height 0.3 to 1.2 m; lens 24 to 40 mm.
- Telescopic crane: telescope at 0.3 to 1.5 m/s for smooth work, cap 2 m/s; full extension 3 to 12 m takes 3 to 6 s minimum, so a hero crane move should be 6 to 15 s. Height 0.3 to 7 m. Ease in and out each 1 to 2 s; avoid harder stops, the specs single out deflection at starts and stops.
- Arm car: camera 0.3 to 1.5 m above road, 2 to 4.3 m from the car centre line, swing at 10 to 60° per second (tip speed up to about 4.5 m/s on top of road speed). Lens 18 to 40 mm. Keep frames settled: no shake, slow orbit while matching the car.
- Robot arm: for whip moves use up to 2 to 4 m/s with ease of about 0.25 s each end (from 1 m in 0.5 s). Use only for very short, fast beats.
- Easing curve: smooth S curve (cubic or quintic). No maker publishes ease times; use 15 to 25 percent of move length per end for slow moves, and at least 1 s per end for crane and arm moves over 3 m.
- Pan and tilt on top of a move: 2 to 15° per second for calm shots. Gimbal limits (360° per second and up) are not a guide.
- Framing defaults for this car: low (0.3 to 0.6 m) and close with 24 to 40 mm for hero shots; 18 mm only for top shots and very close passes; never shake the frame to sell speed.
- Repeat passes: plan moves as keyframed paths with fixed timing so light and reflections line up on each take.
