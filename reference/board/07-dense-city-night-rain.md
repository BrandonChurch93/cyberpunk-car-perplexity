## A dense city at night in rain

What to learn: Depth layers, window variety, color of the cloud base, how far detail survives

### Sources

#### S1 · Low cloud from Victoria Peak at night
- URL: https://victoriapeakhongkong.com/guides/the-peak-after-dark/
- Image: https://victoriapeakhongkong.com/images/uploaded/guides/the-peak-after-dark/body-8.jpg
- Shows: A wet lookout in front of several tiers of Hong Kong towers under low cloud.
- Observations:
  1. The foreground railing is a hard dark silhouette with wet speckle; towers just behind it show separate warm window groups on darker walls.
  2. In the middle tier, outlines and the largest window groups survive, but small facade mullions no longer read through the gray-violet veil.
  3. Some of the tallest buildings lose their top edges in the cloud while their crown lights still show as pale blooms. The distant shore is largely a chain of low glowing marks.
  4. The low cloud over the brightest blocks is pale beige-gray; farther right it cools to mauve-gray. The sky is not a uniform black cutout behind the roofs.

### Real-world sizes

- Office facade module example: panels 1.50 to 2.25 m wide × 4.30 m tall with a 4.30 m floor-to-floor interval. A second case has 1.52 m panel width and 4.20 m floor-to-floor ([CTBUH facade case studies](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/2980-paper32-v2.pdf)).
- Mixed-use tower example: roughly 3.5 m floor-to-floor with 3.0 m clear inside ([CTBUH Vienna tower case study](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/download/2509-the-vienna-donau-city-tower-2000mm-flat-slabs-as-outrigger-structure-for-unique-landmark-building.pdf)).
- Exterior split air-conditioning box: 800 mm wide × 550 mm high × 285 mm deep in two small examples, with an 800 × 714 × 285 mm taller option ([Mitsubishi Electric Hong Kong](https://www.mitsubishielectric.com.hk/en/Split-Type-Air-Conditioner)).
- Hong Kong street tram gauge: 1,067 mm, with an overhead trolley supply. Its narrow street-running gauge is a size reference, not a specification for an elevated rail system ([Hong Kong Transport Department](https://www.td.gov.hk/mini_site/atd/2025/en/section5-12.html)).

### Build notes

- Start with three city layers: nearest facades about 20 to 60 m from the deck, middle massing around 70 to 250 m, and skyline roughly 300 to 1,200 m. They are shot-layout targets. Check that visible roof and facade edges overlap at all camera positions rather than reading as three flat backdrops.
- In front, reserve true window recess and room depth for the nearest row. Start with 1.5 m glazing modules and mix roughly 3.5 m and 4.3 m floor intervals by building type. Group lit and unlit rooms in blocks of 2 to 5 bays, then vary each lit room's level and warmth. Keep room depth and occlusion through the glass visible when the camera moves.
- Let mid-distance fixtures such as balcony units, drainpipes, planted roof masses and sign supports read as silhouettes first. Give the elevated line a real deck and end supports. Keep moving lights on that line, separated by dark gaps, with no detached points in the middle of the facades.
- Tune haze against a common neutral facade patch: as first test values, retain about 80% of clear-air contrast at 50 m, 40% at 200 m and less than 15% at 500 m for a rain-heavy viewing direction. Keep far large signs and crown lights legible after small facade detail disappears. Avoid using defocus to hide missing geometry.
- Build cloud fill as a broad low-lit field with a few unequal colored pockets. Keep most of the cloud base muted and keep the strong warm and cool sources local. In the wide shot, test for a few crown tips entering the cloud and for a clear dark gap between bright side facades.
