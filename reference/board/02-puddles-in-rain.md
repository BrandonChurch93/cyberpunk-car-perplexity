## Puddles in rain

What to learn: Reflection sharpness, ring size and spacing, edges, depth cues

### Sources

#### S1 · Puddles in a Chapel Hill parking lot
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2005-06-06_Puddles_in_Chapel_Hill_parking_lot.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/2005-06-06_Puddles_in_Chapel_Hill_parking_lot.jpg
- Shows: Overcast daytime asphalt parking lot with a row of puddles in low spots.
- Observations:
  1. From the low camera the puddles are stretched sideways, about 6:1 width to depth on screen.
  2. Each puddle has a darker damp band around it, about 10 to 15 percent of the puddle width.
  3. Puddles mirror the trees sharply while the damp band around them shows no image.
  4. The puddles line up along one depression line, not spread at random.
  5. Edges are crisp but irregular. Dry asphalt is light gray, the damp band clearly darker.

#### S8 · Dirty raindrops, top-down view of a muddy puddle
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dirty_Raindrops_(56193806).jpeg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ec/Dirty_Raindrops_%2856193806%29.jpeg
- Shows: Shallow muddy puddle seen from above with many rain rings.
- Observations:
  1. Each ring packet has 3 to 5 concentric crests.
  2. Crest spacing inside a packet is about 8 to 12 percent of the outer radius.
  3. Rings overlap and pass through each other without merging.
  4. The largest rings are about 6 times the diameter of the smallest.
  5. Rings show mostly as light and dark refraction lines; the bottom is visible, so the water is shallow.
  6. Young rings have a bright point at the center from the rebound.

#### S9 · Here comes rain again (thin film and deeper water)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Here_comes_rain_again.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8d/Here_comes_rain_again.jpg

#### S11 · Rain droplets on sky-lit water

### Real-world sizes
- Raindrop diameter 0.1 to 9 mm mean, breaking up at large sizes. Drizzle of 0.5 mm lands at 2 m/s, 5 mm drops at about 9 m/s (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain)
- Rain rate: light under 2.5 mm/h, moderate 2.5 to 7.6 mm/h, heavy over 7.6 mm/h, violent over 50 mm/h (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain)
- Raindrops under about 2 mm are close to spherical; a 3 mm drop falls at about 8 m/s (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindrop)
- Capillary-gravity ripples on water have a minimum phase speed of about 0.23 m/s at a wavelength of about 1.7 cm (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave)
- Capillary length of water about 2.71 mm at 20 °C. Water bodies wider than this flatten under gravity, so a free puddle edge stands only a few mm high (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Water refractive index 1.333 (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)
- Car park deck minimum fall 1:60. Ponding comes from deflection, poor falls and blocked drains; line markings can hold water (source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8124e5274a2e87db6400/enhancing_the_whole_life_structural_performance_of_multi-storey_car_parks.pdf)

### Build notes
- Sharpness: puddle reflection roughness 0.02 to 0.05, near mirror (S2, S4, S5). The wet ground around it should be clearly blurred, 0.15 to 0.3 (S4, S6). The sharp-versus-blurred contrast at the edge is the main cue.
- Brightness: puddle reflection 70 to 100 percent of the direct source (S2, S5). Dark sky reflects black, so puddles at night read as black shapes with bright pieces of signs in them (S4, S5, S7).
- Edges: drive the puddle mask from height (low spots, joints, kerb lines) plus aggregate noise at 5 to 20 mm, so shorelines are jagged and dry stones poke through (S3, S4, S13). Allow straight edges along slab joints and line markings (S6).
- Damp halo: a darker, non-mirror band outside each puddle, about 10 to 15 percent of the puddle width (S1, S13).
- Shoreline rim: a thin bright meniscus line of 2 to 5 mm at grazing view (S5, S13).
- Ring size: with drops at 2 to 5 mm and spacing set by about 1.7 cm ripple wavelength, ring packets should reach about 2 to 15 cm diameter before fading (S12). Packets carry 2 to 6 crests (S8, S10, S11), with the largest rings about 3 to 6 times the size of new ones (S8, S11).
- Ring speed: expand outward at about 0.2 to 0.3 m/s. Lifetime about 0.3 to 0.6 s for deep water (an estimate, not measured), shorter (about half) on thin film, with 1 to 2 crests only (S9).
- Overlap: add rings as summed normals so they pass through each other without merging (S8, S11). Reflection is broken only on crests (S12).
- Density: heavy rain (over 7.6 mm/h) should give about 20 to 30 active rings in a frame-sized patch (S11). Each new ring gets a short bright or dark center dot for the rebound (S8, S11).
- Depth cues: thin water shows aggregate through it (S8, S9, S12); deep water hides it and mirrors fully. Blend the base albedo visibility by water depth over roughly 0 to 3 mm.
- Grazing view: puddles stretch to about 6:1 on screen at low camera height (S1) and rings squash to about 3:1 (S10). Fresnel should push puddle reflection near full at grazing angles.
