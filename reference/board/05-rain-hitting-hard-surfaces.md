## Rain hitting hard surfaces

What to learn: Splash crowns, mist height, timing

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/05-rain-hitting-hard-surfaces/` (`s1.jpg` to `s9.jpg`).

### Sources

#### S1 · Heavy raindrop splash crowns across a wet slate roof (1/125 s, 30 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hard_rain_on_a_roof.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/65/Hard_rain_on_a_roof.jpg
- Shows: Hundreds of raindrop impacts across wet dark slate roof tiles filmed at 1/125 s, f/3.7, 30 mm (cache s1.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Even in heavy rain, open splash crowns sit 10 to 30 cm apart in any single 8 ms frame because each crown persists for only about 15 to 35 ms before collapsing.
  2. Thin-film crowns on hard slate form flared conical cups wider than tall, roughly 15 to 35 mm wide at the rim and 8 to 20 mm tall, with walls tilted 25 to 40 degrees outward from vertical.
  3. At 1/125 s (8 ms), fast secondary micro-droplets leaving the crown rim blur into fine white radial streaks 10 to 30 mm long shooting upward at 35 to 60 degrees above the tile plane.
  4. Better-drained upper tile zones with very thin water film produce flat star-shaped prompt splashes with minimal wall height, while thicker film pooled along tile overlap seams yields taller, cleaner cups.

#### S2 · Edge-on view of rooftop splash crowns and bounce mist layer (1/125 s, 146 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_1.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_1.jpg
- Shows: Telephoto grazing view at 1/125 s, f/5.0, 146 mm of rain hitting a flat roof deck in front of a white metal fascia trim strip and dark background (cache s2.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Against the dark background and ~80 to 100 mm tall white fascia trim, secondary bounce droplets create a continuous airborne mist band hugging the hard deck.
  2. Mist density is highest in the bottom 3 to 8 cm above the surface, while sparser ballistic bounce droplets arc up to 12 to 20 cm high (about 1.2 to 2 times the fascia trim height) before falling back.
  3. Foreground thin-film crowns show a sharp dark circular base ring 12 to 25 mm wide where the expanding cavity clears the reflective water film, surmounted by a bright translucent V-shaped wall 10 to 22 mm high.
  4. Airborne bounce droplets trace short curved parabolic dashes 4 to 15 mm long tilted 30 to 65 degrees from vertical during the 8 ms exposure.

#### S3 · Fast-shutter rooftop crowns and frozen secondary spray (1/320 s, 146 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_2.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_2.jpg
- Shows: Fast-shutter 1/320 s, f/5.0, 146 mm telephoto frame of heavy rain hitting a flat roof membrane (cache s3.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At 1/320 s (3.1 ms), airborne bounce spray freezes into thousands of crisp white micro-beads 0.3 to 1.5 mm in diameter hovering over the deck.
  2. The hero foreground crown slightly right of center displays thin-film crown geometry clearly: a dark oval base collar (~25 to 30 mm across), a glassy flared wall ~15 to 20 mm tall, and 10 to 14 jagged rim cusps pinching droplets from their tips.
  3. Toward the rear left of the deck where depth of field softens and splashes overlap in depth, individual micro-beads blend into a pale grey-white mist layer 5 to 15 cm high.
  4. Primary incoming raindrops in the upper dark band appear as straighter, fainter diagonal lines (~12 degrees off vertical), whereas secondary bounce spray below 15 cm sprays symmetrically left and right at wide angles.

#### S4 · Slower-shutter rooftop splash baskets and radial ejecta fans (1/100 s, 146 mm)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_5.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1d/Rain_splashes_on_rooftop_5.jpg
- Shows: Rooftop downpour photographed at 1/100 s, f/5.0, 146 mm, close to the 1/60 s shutter duration of 30 fps cinema (cache s4.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. At 1/100 s (10 ms), crown rim ligaments and departing droplets smear radially into fan-shaped white baskets 20 to 40 mm wide and 15 to 28 mm tall.
  2. Brightness concentrates along the left and right grazing edges of each funnel and at the upward cusp rays, while the hollow interior stays 20 to 40 percent opaque so dark deck reflections remain visible inside the cup.
  3. Along the foreground seam where water pools slightly deeper (1 to 2 mm), impacts surround the crown base with one or two tight bright shock rings 20 to 45 mm wide that are absent on thinner surrounding film.
  4. Along the far edge of the deck, overlapping splash fans and micro-droplets sum into a soft mist cushion 8 to 15 cm tall that lifts background black levels by 15 to 25 percent.

#### S5 · Rain impacting wet pavement film and shallow puddles side by side (1/125 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pflaster_im_Regen_(fcm).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/27/Pflaster_im_Regen_%28fcm%29.jpg
- Shows: Wet dark pavement during steady rain at 1/125 s, f/4.0 showing thin-film high spots alongside shallow pooled puddles (cache s5.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Water film thickness splits splash behavior sharply within a single meter of pavement: sub-millimeter wet stone shows only brief low crowns and faint rings under 30 mm, while puddles deeper than 2 to 3 mm produce tall central stalks and wide concentric wave trains.
  2. Inside the puddles, cavity collapse drives vertical Worthington rebound stalks 12 to 25 mm straight up that read as sharp dark spikes against bright sky reflections.
  3. Several puddle impacts leave hemispherical air bubbles 5 to 12 mm wide floating on the surface after crown collapse.
  4. Overlapping ring waves cover 70 to 90 percent of the pooled areas and slice reflected overhead branches into horizontal ribbons 3 to 10 mm thick while preserving large-scale light and dark reflection zones.

#### S6 · Expanding rain ring waves in an asphalt puddle beside a manhole cover (1/320 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_on_a_puddle_in_Tuntorp_1.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/01/Rain_on_a_puddle_in_Tuntorp_1.jpg
- Shows: Shallow rainwater puddle on rough asphalt next to a cast-iron manhole cover at 1/320 s, f/6.3, 146 mm (cache s6.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Scaling against the ~600 mm diameter manhole cover on the right, fresh inner impact rings measure 15 to 30 mm across and mature expanding ring sets reach 80 to 180 mm diameter before damping below visibility.
  2. Each raindrop impact launches a group packet of 3 to 6 concentric crests with a dominant wavelength of 8 to 16 mm, preceded on the outside by fainter 3 to 5 mm capillary ripples.
  3. Intersecting ring packets pass linearly through each other without breaking, forming bright diamond-shaped specular glints where two crest slopes add constructively toward the sky.
  4. Within 1 to 2 cm of the rough asphalt shoreline where water depth tapers below 1 mm, ring amplitude damps to zero with almost no reflected wave returning from the puddle edge.

#### S7 · Macro impact cavity, crown wall, and rim ligament breakup
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2006-02-13_Drop-impact.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/2006-02-13_Drop-impact.jpg
- Shows: High-speed side-view macro photograph through glass showing both the subsurface impact hemisphere and the airborne crown rim pinching off droplets (cache s7.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Impact excavates a hemispherical crater roughly 3 to 4 drop diameters wide while lofting a thin liquid crown wall around its perimeter.
  2. Plateau-Rayleigh rim instability splits the upper crown edge into discrete vertical finger ligaments spaced every 25 to 45 degrees around the rim, each pinching off 1 to 3 spherical secondary droplets.
  3. The liquid sheet is thickest at its curved base fillet and thinnest just below the rim, creating bright vertical specular flutes and crisp dark refractive edges.
  4. Secondary droplets detach at upward-outward angles of 15 to 45 degrees from vertical with diameters equal to roughly 15 to 35 percent of the impacting drop.

#### S8 · Macro central Worthington rebound stalk and early ring train
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2006-01-28_drop-impact_backjet.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/08/2006-01-28_drop-impact_backjet.jpg
- Shows: High-speed macro photograph of the central vertical rebound column rising after crown collapse inside concentric ripples (cache s8.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. In water deeper than ~2 mm, inward collapse of the impact crater (occurring after ~15 ms) shoots a smooth cylindrical rebound column 4 to 7 drop radii high and roughly 1 drop diameter thick straight up along the surface normal.
  2. By peak stalk height, the earlier outer crown has already subsided into a disturbed annular trough 2 to 3 stalk heights wide, surrounded by 3 to 4 clean concentric wave crests.
  3. Small satellite droplets from the earlier crown phase are already high in flight well outside the ring zone when the central stalk reaches its apex, proving the two-stage timing sequence (crown first, stalk second).
  4. Curved ring slopes focus bright refractive caustics onto the substrate below while creating alternating light and dark bands in reflection.

#### S9 · Rebound drop pinch-off inside a dispersing capillary-gravity ring packet (1/250 s)
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_drop_impact_on_a_water-surface_-_(1).jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Water_drop_impact_on_a_water-surface_-_%281%29.jpg
- Shows: Macro photograph at 1/250 s, f/9.0, 100 mm of a slender rebound stalk pinching off a single spherical drop inside five concentric rings (cache s9.jpg).
- Observations:
  1. Near the end of stalk rise (~20 to 30 ms post-impact), surface tension pinches the tip of the tapered column into a thin neck, detaching one spherical secondary drop roughly 0.8 to 1.2 times the original drop diameter.
  2. Five concentric wave crests extend outward to radii 3 to 8 times the stalk height, with shorter-wavelength capillary ripples leading on the outer edge of the packet and a broader gravity-capillary trough trailing inside.
  3. The spherical detached drop acts as a tiny convex lens, concentrating bright background light at its lower edge with a dark refractive ring around its upper contour.
  4. Beyond the fifth outer ripple crest, the surrounding water surface remains completely flat, marking a clean outward-propagating group front.

### Real-world sizes
- Impacting raindrop diameters and terminal velocities at sea level: 1.0 mm drops hit at 4.03 m/s; 1.6 mm (moderate rain median) at 5.65 m/s; 2.0 mm (heavy rain median) at 6.49 m/s; 3.0 mm at 8.06 m/s; 4.6 mm at ~8.95 m/s; 5.0 mm at 9.09 m/s. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle and https://www.madsci.org/posts/archives/2000-07/962626446.Ph.r.html)
- Thin-film splash crown height and width (Parmentier, Terrapon, and Gilet 2023, Phys. Rev. Fluids 8, 053603, for `r_d = 2.3 mm` / `4.6 mm` diameter drops on `65 to 500 um` water films): maximum crown height is `h / r_d = 3 to 10` (`6 to 22 mm` physical height); crown top radius is `r_t / r_d = 5 to 9` (`10 to 20 mm` radius, so `20 to 40 mm` crown top width); crown base radius is `r_c / r_d = 4 to 8` (`8 to 18 mm` radius, so `16 to 36 mm` base width). Films thinner than `100 um` tear rapidly into fine mist droplets with minimal wall, while films thicker than `100 um` form coherent flared crowns. (source: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://physics.aps.org/articles/v16/86)
- Splash crown and cavity lifetime: governed by capillary timescale `t_c = sqrt(4 * rho * r_d^3 / (3 * gamma))`, which is ~15 ms for a 4.6 mm drop (peak crown height and radius reached at `14.0 +/- 1.4 ms` to `16.4 +/- 2.7 ms`). For 3.0 to 3.6 mm drops hitting water, crown height peaks at `8.9 ms`, coherent crown shape breaks down after `~12 ms`, maximum cavity depth occurs at `17.9 ms`, and primary splash structures subside by `33.9 to 35 ms` (~0.5 to 1.0 frames at 30 fps). (source: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9103484/)
- Splash stalk and secondary bounce mist height: 2.74 mm raindrops impacting water produce a dense bright bottom splash layer averaging `14.85 mm` (~15 mm) tall, while ejected secondary micro-droplets (diameters mostly under `0.9 mm`, up to `1.8 mm`) loft a surface mist layer reaching up to `0.4 m` (40 cm) max height, with field roof photos S2 to S4 showing dense visual mist in the lowest `3 to 15 cm` and sparse ballistic droplets to `20 cm`. (source: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10511775)
- Ring wave speed on shallow water and puddles: finite-depth gravity-capillary dispersion is `omega^2 = (g * k + (sigma / rho) * k^3) * tanh(k * h)` (`g = 9.81 m/s2`, `sigma = 0.073 N/m`, `rho = 1000 kg/m3`). In the shallow-water gravity limit (`k * h << 1`), non-dispersive wave speed is `v = sqrt(g * h)`, giving `0.14 m/s` at `h = 2 mm` depth, `0.22 m/s` at `h = 5 mm`, `0.31 m/s` at `h = 10 mm`, and `0.44 m/s` at `h = 20 mm`. Including surface tension, gravity-capillary waves have a minimum phase speed of `0.23 m/s` (23 cm/s) at `lambda = 17 mm`, while shorter `3 to 8 mm` capillary ripples travel faster at `0.30 to 0.50 m/s` (with group speed `v_g = 1.5 * v_p`), placing fine capillary ripples on the leading outside edge of each expanding ring packet. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17098v2)

### Build notes
- Damp or ultra-thin film surfaces (`< 0.1 mm` water depth, drained asphalt high spots and sloped bodywork): spawn tiny prompt-splash radial micro-fans (`8 to 15 mm` wide, `4 to 8 mm` tall) with zero expanding ring waves (S1, S5).
- Wet thin-film hard surfaces (`0.1 to 1.5 mm` water depth, flat wet concrete/asphalt, parapet coping, flat roof and hood): spawn flared conical basket crowns `16 to 36 mm` wide at the rim and `8 to 22 mm` tall, tilted `25 to 38 degrees` outward from the surface normal, with radial motion-blurred white cusp rays; because physical crown lifetime is `15 to 35 ms` against a `16.7 ms` shutter (`30 fps`, `180 degree`), render each active crown for 1 frame (occasionally decaying into a 2nd frame), never lingering across many frames (S1, S2, S4).
- Standing puddles (`> 2 mm` water depth): follow a 1-frame impact crown with a 1-frame vertical Worthington rebound stalk (`12 to 22 mm` tall, `2 to 3 mm` wide) plus an expanding concentric normal-map ring packet (S5, S6, S8).
- Drive puddle ring wave expansion at `0.22 to 0.35 m/s` radial speed (`sqrt(g * h)` bounded by the `0.23 m/s` capillary-gravity floor): spawn 3 to 5 concentric crests per impact with `8 to 16 mm` dominant wavelength (plus a subtle `4 mm` outer capillary precursor), fade ring amplitude exponentially over `0.4 to 0.8 s` so maximum ring diameter caps at `100 to 180 mm`, sum overlapping rings linearly in slope space, and ramp ring amplitude smoothly to zero where puddle depth tapers below `1.5 mm` within `1 to 2 cm` of puddle shorelines (S6).
- Add a low ground-skimming bounce-mist layer over hard horizontal surfaces where rain is heaviest (open deck bays, parapet cap, vehicle roof and rear deck): concentrate soft volumetric or card mist in the bottom `0.03 to 0.12 m` above the surface with exponential vertical falloff vanishing by `0.20 to 0.35 m`, lit strongly by grazing headlamp, tail-lamp, and low signage light (S2, S3, S4).
- Match active splash spatial density to heavy-rain flux times crown duration: at `~500 drops/m2/s` (heavy rain, `2.05 mm` median drop) and a `0.020 s` visible crown lifetime, roughly `10 active crowns per m2` exist in any single `1/60 s` frame (`~30 cm` average spacing between active crowns, matching S1 to S4), with only the largest 20 to 30 percent (`> 2.2 mm` drops) producing prominent `15 to 25 mm` baskets.
