## Wet asphalt and concrete at night under colored light

What to learn: Darkening, gloss, how color light spreads, how texture shows through water

### Sources

#### S1 · Nanjing Road, wet paving under shop signs
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NanjingRoad3.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/NanjingRoad3.jpg
- Shows: Wet pedestrian paving at night with pink, cyan and orange sign light on it.
- Observations:
  1. Each sign reflects as a vertical streak that runs toward the camera. The streaks are 3 to 5 times longer than the source is wide.
  2. Streaks are cut into separate vertical bands by the paving joints. Between bands the surface is dark.
  3. Reflected streak brightness is about half that of the sign itself.
  4. The warm facade light spreads as a wide orange wash over the paving. The small saturated signs (magenta, cyan) keep their hue in the streaks.
  5. People reflect as dark vertical shapes below their feet, so the wet ground also mirrors dark objects, not only lights.

#### S2 · Kerala road at night with colored tree uplights
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Night_view_1.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Night_view_1.jpg
- Shows: Wet asphalt road with green and purple uplit trees and sodium street lamps.
- Observations:
  1. Green and purple uplights reflect as long vertical streaks on the road at the right of frame.
  2. In the foreground the streaks break up into separate bright speckles, one per aggregate stone.
  3. Sodium lamps give an orange tint to the whole wet road, wider than the lamp reflections themselves.
  4. The kerb is darker than the road surface and shows almost no reflection.
  5. A car roof in frame carries water drops that read as a field of tiny point highlights.

#### S3 · Tokyo street in heavy rain
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:54525452215_-_Flickr_-_mirrors_are_more_fun.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/19/54525452215_-_Flickr_-_mirrors_are_more_fun.jpg
- Shows: Asphalt road at night in heavy rain with traffic lights and headlights.
- Observations:
  1. Red and green traffic light reflections run as columns from near the horizon almost to the camera.
  2. Each column is about 1.5 to 2 times wider than its lamp.
  3. Asphalt between columns is near black, with a fine granular sparkle.
  4. Lane markings are barely visible in the wet; they do not stand out as they would dry.
  5. Where rain is hitting at the right of frame the reflections get a speckled, broken texture.

### Real-world sizes
- Fresh asphalt albedo: 0.04; worn asphalt: 0.12; new concrete: 0.55 (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo)
- Wet darkening: light trapped in the water film by total internal reflection is sent back into the surface, so it gets more chances to be absorbed (source: https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-27-7-1278)
- Wet albedo falls off nonlinearly. Dark surfaces lose more relative brightness, and wet color is more saturated (source: https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/042ea1c8-2da0-481b-8944-7b729ada303c/content)
- Water refractive index 1.333; water to air critical angle 48.6 degrees (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)
- Reflection rises toward mirror-like as the angle nears grazing (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_equations). For n = 1.333, normal incidence reflectance works out to about 2 percent (calculated, not quoted).
- Pavement macrotexture wavelength 0.5 to 50 mm; microtexture 1 µm to 0.5 mm (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/t504036.cfm)
- Mean texture depth: dense graded asphalt 0.4 to 0.6 mm, stone matrix asphalt over 1.0 mm, open graded friction course 1.5 to 3.0 mm (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/14065/004.cfm)
- Concrete mean texture depth at least 0.8 mm; tining grooves about 3 mm wide and 3 mm deep (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/pubs/hif17011.pdf)
- Car park deck minimum fall 1:60. Line markings can block drainage and cause ponding (source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8124e5274a2e87db6400/enhancing_the_whole_life_structural_performance_of_multi-storey_car_parks.pdf)

### Build notes
- Darkening: multiply wet concrete albedo by about 0.5 to 0.6 and wet asphalt by about 0.6 to 0.7, then raise saturation of the base color by 10 to 20 percent. Dry asphalt base about 0.04 to 0.12 linear, concrete about 0.3 to 0.55.
- Gloss: wet porous surface roughness about 0.15 to 0.3 (film over aggregate), puddles 0.02 to 0.05, dry asphalt 0.8 to 0.9. Keep Fresnel F0 at about 0.02 for the water layer.
- Reflection length: light streaks should run toward the camera 3 to 5 times the source height (S1, S9) and be 1.5 to 2 times the source width (S3). If they come out round, the roughness is too isotropic. Stretch the lobe along the view direction or use anisotropic blur on the planar reflection.
- Texture through water: break streaks with a high frequency aggregate mask (5 to 20 mm features) that changes roughness, not only albedo, so streaks turn into speckle near the camera (S2, S6, S7) and smooth out with distance (S7).
- Color spread: large signs give wide soft blobs with no readable text (S4, S12). Small saturated lamps give narrow columns that keep their hue (S9). Blur radius should grow with roughness and distance from the surface.
- Unlit wet ground goes near black (S10, S12). Do not lift it with ambient light. Most of the read comes from reflected light.
- Painted lines: roughness about 0.4 to 0.5 when wet, albedo 0.6 to 0.8. They should stay lighter and duller than the asphalt (S4, S5, S12) and keep their own hue under colored light.
- Joints and gaps: dark lines that cut reflection columns into segments (S1, S11).
- Rain on the film: add moving ripple normals and small bright splash points inside lit areas only (S3, S10).
