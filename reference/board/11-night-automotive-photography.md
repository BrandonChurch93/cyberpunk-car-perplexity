## Night automotive photography and film

What to learn: Lighting setups, lens choices, bokeh shape, flare, where black sits in the grade

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/night-automotive-photography/` (n01 to n11, fg-batman-b08, fg-batman-b23; n02 and n10 viewed but not used as sources).

### Sources

#### S1 · Drive, ASC article on night car work
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/drive-road-warriors/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Cinematographer Newton Thomas Sigel's account of lighting and lenses for the night driving in Drive.
- Observations:
  1. Exposure: Alexa rated at 800 ASA, pushed to 1600 when needed, with night work around T2.8.
  2. Lenses: mostly an Angenieux Optimo 15-40 mm zoom, Zeiss Master Primes for night car interiors, and the director favored 18 mm and 21 mm. Wide lenses close to the car are the norm.
  3. Car lighting: a roof speed rail "halo" rig of 150 W Arri tungsten units gelled with Rosco Urban Color or Lee 241 to match sodium and mercury street lights, plus LED ribbon sticks of 3, 6 and 12 in, one 12 in stick on the windshield for red and green traffic light effects, all on a 12 V 150 Ah battery with 32 dimmer channels, run by wireless DMX from a follow van.
  4. Wide exteriors were backlit with 16 head and 9 head Night Lights, 4 ft tungsten balloons for fill and real cobra head sodium street lamps; the on-set grade only added some contrast and saturation.

#### S2 · Collateral, ASC article on lighting the city at night
- URL: https://theasc.com/magazine/aug04/collateral/page4.html
- Image: none used
- Shows: Dion Beebe and Paul Cameron on lighting Los Angeles night exteriors and the taxi.
- Observations:
  1. The rule on night exteriors was "make the fill light the key light": very little added light, matched to the existing city glow.
  2. Soft sources: a Kino Flo Image 80 with ND 0.6 and 2 to 3 layers of muslin to get a low, soft level.
  3. Practical color matching: mercury vapor imitated with 1/4 CTS plus 1/2 Plus Green, sodium with Light Amber plus 1/2 Plus Green. The night city is green and amber, not blue.
  4. Lighting balloons were dropped because they lit the street too much; cameras ran with gain (Viper up to +9 dB, F900 up to +12 dB) and DigiPrime lenses down to T1.3, framed 2.37:1.

#### S3 · Collateral, Hell on Wheels, exposure numbers
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/hell-on-wheels-collateral/
- Image: none used
- Shows: The technical breakdown of exposure levels and cab lighting for Collateral.
- Observations:
  1. Faces were held at 20 IRE at +3 dB or 30 IRE at +6 dB minimum, which the article says is about 2 1/3 and 1 1/2 stops under middle grey (about 55 IRE). Lower than that showed noise.
  2. The background often "barely registered on the waveform": the city behind sits close to black, but faces keep a clean signal.
  3. The cab was lit with about 30 electroluminescent panels of about 8 by 15 in per cab, dimmable to about 20 percent without color shift, in "a slightly cool green".
  4. Final office sequence was shot at levels "you could barely see with the naked eye", with actors often silhouetted against city reflections in glass, helped by Kino Flo wands wrapped in bobbinet.

#### S4 · The Batman, ASC article on night, rain and lenses
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/greig-fraser-batman/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Greig Fraser on the night look, the rain car chase and the lens choices of The Batman.
- Observations:
  1. Main lenses were Arri Rental Alfa large format anamorphics, wide open at T2.2 where they flared heavily and had low contrast; stopping down 1/2 to 1 stop cleaned the image up.
  2. Blacks were kept "open and dirtier" in the grade to avoid losing detail to contrast; the film was recorded out to 2254 intermediate with bleach bypass for highlight halation.
  3. The Gotham Square Garden night exterior used a soft box of 687 Digital Sputnik modules over a 200 by 170 ft area with Magic Cloth and 251 diffusion.
  4. For the car chase, road and cars were wet down and rain added later; optical flats with clear silicone globules in front of the lens gave controlled drops, lighter for headlights into lens and heavier when there was no direct light. Crash cameras used rehoused Soviet lenses with an elliptical aperture cut to fake anamorphic bokeh, plus Atlas Orion anamorphics.

#### S5 · The Batman, frame from Gotham Square Garden exterior
- URL: https://film-grab.com/2022/07/11/the-batman/
- Image: https://film-grab.com/wp-content/uploads/photo-gallery/The_Batman008.jpg
- Shows: A wet, empty intersection at night in front of the arena with signs and LED screens. (cache fg-batman-b08)
- Observations:
  1. The wet asphalt holds long vertical streaks of teal, warm white and amber from the signs and lights; the road is mid-dark grey with texture everywhere, not black.
  2. Darkest areas are the trees and under the canopy, still showing shape. Nothing reads as pure black.
  3. Highlights (sign letters, street lamps) are bright but small and do not bloom much; skin of the scene stays low contrast.
  4. Saturated color is confined to the signs and their reflections; buildings and road stay near neutral grey-green.

#### S7 · Cooke Optics, what anamorphic lenses do
- URL: https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/an-introduction-to-anamorphic-lenses/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Lens maker explanation of squeeze, bokeh and flare.
- Observations:
  1. A 2x anamorphic squeezes the horizontal axis to 0.5x; a 50 mm keeps the vertical view of a 50 mm but has the horizontal view of a 25 mm.
  2. Out of focus highlights become elliptical; with 2x anamorphics the oval is 50 percent as wide as it is tall.
  3. Anamorphic flares appear as horizontal streaks and elliptical orbs; blue streaks are the best known color, set by coatings.
  4. Lower squeeze factors (1.3x, 1.5x, 1.8x on full frame) give the same traits more weakly; rear anamorphic designs do not show these flares.

#### S16 · Shot Craft, ASC on car commercial lighting
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/shot-craft-the-art-of-the-ad/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Cinematographers on how car spots are lit and lensed.
- Observations:
  1. For an Audi spot at night in a riverbed, Paul Cameron stretched 120 ft of silver lamé between two construction cranes, rippled with fans, and bounced four or five 7K xenon lamps into it to make highlights look like liquid mercury.
  2. Car work relies on huge flown bounce surfaces over the car to place one long shaped highlight along the body.
  3. Christopher Probst names anamorphic lenses, even cropped to 16:9, as a way to get cinematic bokeh.
  4. Vintage anamorphics cropped for 16:9 delivery are a normal commercial choice.

#### S17 · Spectre, ASC on the Rome night car chase
- URL: https://theasc.com/article/sinister-sect-spectre/
- Image: none used
- Shows: Hoyte van Hoytema on lenses and car interiors for the night chase in Rome.
- Observations:
  1. Workhorse lenses were Arri Master Anamorphics, T1.9 across the set, chosen to avoid extreme headlight flares and to shoot in low light.
  2. Cameras were mounted to the cars and a Russian Arm was used often, to keep frames "elegant and settled" while conveying speed.
  3. Car interiors on stage used rear screen projection, LED panels, rotating mirrors and moving panels for passing light.
  4. Bright low resolution LED screens fed with plates were used off camera as light sources, because they give real reflections on the car and light on faces.

### Real-world sizes
- 2x anamorphic bokeh: oval 50 percent as wide as tall; a 50 mm 2x anamorphic has the horizontal view of a 25 mm. (source: https://cookeoptics.com/news-and-events/an-introduction-to-anamorphic-lenses/)
- Night face exposure in Collateral: 20 to 30 IRE, about 1 1/2 to 2 1/3 stops under middle grey at 55 IRE. (source: https://theasc.com/article/hell-on-wheels-collateral/)
- Drive night stop: about T2.8 at 800 to 1600 ASA; lenses 15 to 40 mm, favored 18 and 21 mm. (source: https://theasc.com/article/drive-road-warriors/)
- The Batman lenses wide open: T2.2 anamorphic. (source: https://theasc.com/article/greig-fraser-batman/)
- Spectre night chase lenses: T1.9 anamorphic. (source: https://theasc.com/article/sinister-sect-spectre/)
- Car roof halo lights in Drive: 150 W tungsten; LED sticks 3, 6 and 12 in. (source: https://theasc.com/article/drive-road-warriors/)
- Collateral cab panels: about 30 panels of 8 by 15 in per cab. (source: https://theasc.com/article/hell-on-wheels-collateral/)
- Silver lamé bounce for a night car spot: 120 ft long, four or five 7K xenons. (source: https://theasc.com/article/shot-craft-the-art-of-the-ad/)
- Batman night soft box: 200 by 170 ft, 687 LED modules. (source: https://theasc.com/article/greig-fraser-batman/)

### Build notes
- Exposure: key the car and subject around 1.5 to 2.3 stops under middle grey (scene linear about 0.04 to 0.06 if mid grey is 0.18); let backgrounds sit 2 to 4 stops lower again.
- Black: lift the darkest shadow to about 2 to 4 percent display (not 0) and keep texture visible on asphalt and under trees. True 0 only under the car and in the deepest gaps. Blacks neutral or slightly green-cool, never saturated.
- Color: neutral dark grey for structure; saturation only in signs, tail lamps and their reflections. Practical street light colors: sodium amber and mercury green, not pure blue.
- Paint on a dark car: show the body through 1 to 3 long highlight streaks from a large soft source above (a flown bounce), not through general fill.
- Lens: default 2x anamorphic look on 35 to 40 mm equivalent for coverage, 18 to 24 mm for close and low car shots, stop T2 to T2.8.
- Bokeh: vertical ovals, width 0.5 of height for 2x (0.67 for 1.5x). Disc edge slightly brighter; size scales with defocus. Apply the same oval to rain drops on the lens.
- Flare: horizontal streak only for the strongest 1 to 3 sources (headlights, the brightest sign), length 30 to 100 percent of frame width, thickness 0.2 to 0.5 percent of frame height, cool blue tint, intensity a few percent of source. Add 2 to 4 faint elliptical ghosts on the line through frame center. No streaks for ordinary signage.
- Halation and bloom: soft warm bloom around the brightest highlights only, radius 0.5 to 2 percent of frame height.
- Lens water: an optional layer of fixed drops on the lens, fewer drops when a headlight points into the lens.
- Moving light: for interior or close shots, animate passing light bands across the car at road speed.
