## Water on glossy coated paint, macro

What to learn: Bead shape, size spread, refraction, highlights, merging, trails

Cached copies: `reference/cache/board/water-on-paint-macro/` (a01 to a16; a06, a07, a08, a10, a14, a15 not used).

### Sources

#### S1 · Black sedan beaded in steady rain
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_beads_cover_the_surface_of_a_black_car_parked_outside_as_rain_falls_steadily.jpg
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Water_beads_cover_the_surface_of_a_black_car_parked_outside_as_rain_falls_steadily.jpg/1280px-Water_beads_cover_the_surface_of_a_black_car_parked_outside_as_rain_falls_steadily.jpg
- Shows: Roof edge, A pillar, door and mirror of a black car covered in small beads while rain falls. (cache a01)
- Observations:
  1. At this viewing distance each bead reads only as a tiny white specular point on black. The paint between beads stays near black, so the wet look comes from a dense field of points, not from a grey sheen.
  2. Bead density is highest on the near-flat roof edge and A pillar, roughly 1 to 3 mm beads almost touching. The mirror cap has fewer, smaller beads, about half the density of the roof.
  3. The chrome window trim carries the same bead texture, but there the beads read as darker dots against the bright chrome. Bead contrast flips sign with the brightness of the surface under it.
  4. The side glass behind is hazed by much smaller drops and film, so glass reads lighter grey and softer than the beaded paint. Paint beads are crisp, glass water is a veil.

#### S2 · Beads on red paint at grazing angle, Honolulu
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beads_(36938855796).jpg
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fa/Beads_%2836938855796%29.jpg/1280px-Beads_%2836938855796%29.jpg
- Shows: Close macro of water beads packed across a red painted surface, viewed nearly edge-on. (cache a02)
- Observations:
  1. At this grazing angle beads are flattened ellipses, about 3 to 4 times wider than tall in the image. Each has a bright white rim highlight along its far (upper) edge and a darker, more saturated red core.
  2. Bead bodies are deeper red than the dry paint around them. Refraction through the lens of water shows darker paint, so beads are darker than paint except for their highlight.
  3. Large beads are 3 to 5 times the diameter of the smallest ones. Tiny satellite beads, well under 1 mm, fill nearly every gap between large ones.
  4. Depth of field is a band about 20 percent of frame height. Out-of-focus beads in front and behind turn into soft red blobs with a soft pale highlight, never into hard dots.

#### S3 · Raindrops on a well polished red roof
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raindrops_on_a_well-polished_car_roof.jpg
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Raindrops_on_a_well-polished_car_roof.jpg/1280px-Raindrops_on_a_well-polished_car_roof.jpg
- Shows: A red car roof after rain with a white car reflected in the paint beyond. (cache a03)
- Observations:
  1. The size spread is bimodal: many small round beads around 1 to 2 mm, plus large irregular puddle beads around 10 to 20 mm made by merging.
  2. Merged beads take peanut, kidney and multi-lobe shapes. The outline keeps the lobes of the original beads, so a merged bead is never a clean ellipse.
  3. Large puddle beads have flat tops that mirror the sky like the paint itself, with a thin bright outline where the edge curves. Inside they are almost the same color as the paint.
  4. Beads near the roof edge, where the surface curves away, are stretched along the slope, while beads on the flat middle stay round.

#### S4 · Water drops on a white roof, top down
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_drops_on_car_roof.jpg
- Image: https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Water_drops_on_car_roof.jpg/1280px-Water_drops_on_car_roof.jpg
- Shows: White car roof seen nearly straight down, covered in drops of many sizes. (cache a04)
- Observations:
  1. Seen from above, drops are circles or slightly irregular circles. The center of each drop is the same light grey as the paint around it.
  2. Each drop has a dark crescent on one side only, the side away from the light. That crescent is refraction of the dark surroundings through the curved edge, and it is the main thing that makes a drop visible on light paint.
  3. Sizes run from about 0.3 mm to about 6 mm in one frame. Small drops are far more numerous, roughly 5 to 10 small drops for each large one.
  4. There are no trails here; the roof is flat and all drops are static.

### Real-world sizes
- Water capillary length at 20 °C: 2.71 mm. Drops wider than this are flattened by gravity into puddles; smaller drops stay near spherical caps. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Height of a large flattened puddle bead: about 2 × 2.71 mm × sin(θ/2), so about 4.2 mm at a 100° contact angle, derived from the capillary length on the same page. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Contact angle of water on commercial car waxes in one study: 71.17° to 94.85°. Hydrophobic means 90° to 150°, superhydrophobic above 150°. (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12694507/)
- Contact angle classes from a coatings maker: low repellent 70° to 80°, water repellent above 90°, a coating layer measured at 101°. Beads act as small lenses, which is why they leave water spots. (source: https://www.threebond.co.jp/en/technical/technicalnews/detail/pdf/TechnicalNews87_EN-181126.pdf)
- Bead diameters seen on real car paint: about 0.3 mm to 6 mm for single beads, 10 to 20 mm for merged puddle beads. (estimates from S3 and S4 photos: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raindrops_on_a_well-polished_car_roof.jpg and https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_drops_on_car_roof.jpg)

### Build notes
- Bead shape: spherical cap with contact angle 95° to 110° for waxed or coated paint. Above 2.7 mm diameter, flatten the top so height caps near 4 mm.
- Size spread: use a power law or two lobes. About 70 percent of beads 0.3 to 1.5 mm, 25 percent 1.5 to 6 mm, 5 percent merged 8 to 20 mm. Tiny beads fill gaps at 1 to 2 diameters spacing.
- Merged beads: union of 2 to 4 overlapping circles with a soft min blend, so lobes stay visible. Never a clean ellipse.
- Refraction: bead body shows the surface under it shifted and darker than the dry paint (about 0.6 to 0.8 of paint luminance on colored paint). On light paint draw a dark crescent on the side away from the key light; on black paint the body is near black.
- Highlights: one sharp specular point or short arc per bead, on the side toward the light, 5 to 15 percent of bead radius in size. On black paint at distance, beads become only these points.
- Overcast or soft signage light: widen the highlight to a soft band, drop contrast about half.
- Grazing view: squash beads 3 to 4 to 1 and put a bright rim along the far edge.
- Trails: on slopes above about 10°, give large beads a teardrop with tail pointing uphill. Draw sliding paths as clear lanes 1 to 2 bead widths wide with a string of 0.2 to 0.5 mm beads along the edges.
- Pooling: increase density 1.5 to 2 times along gutters, roof rails and lower panel edges.
- Depth of field: at macro framing, keep the sharp band to about 20 percent of frame height; out of focus beads become soft color blobs with a faint pale highlight.
