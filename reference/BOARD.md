## Wet asphalt and concrete at night under colored light

What to learn: Darkening, gloss, how color light spreads, how texture shows through water

### Sources

#### S1 · Nanjing Road, wet paving under shop signs
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NanjingRoad3.jpg
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/NanjingRoad3.jpg
- Shows: Wet pedestrian paving at night with pink, cyan and orange sign light on it.
- Observations:
  1. Each sign reflects as a vertical streak that runs toward the camera. The streaks are 3 to 5 times longer than the source is wide.
  2. Streaks are cut into separate vertical bands by the paving joints. Between bands the surface is dark.
  3. Reflected streak brightness is about half that of the sign itself.
  4. The warm facade light spreads as a wide orange wash over the paving. The small saturated signs (magenta, cyan) keep their hue in the streaks.
  5. People reflect as dark vertical shapes below their feet, so the wet ground also mirrors dark objects, not only lights.

### Real-world sizes
- Fresh asphalt albedo: 0.04; worn asphalt: 0.12; new concrete: 0.55 (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo)
- Wet darkening: light trapped in the water film by total internal reflection is sent back into the surface, so it gets more chances to be absorbed (source: https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-27-7-1278)
- Wet albedo falls off nonlinearly. Dark surfaces lose more relative brightness, and wet color is more saturated (source: https://diglib.eg.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/042ea1c8-2da0-481b-8944-7b729ada303c/content)
- Water refractive index 1.333; water to air critical angle 48.6 degrees (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)
- Reflection rises toward mirror-like as the angle nears grazing (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_equations). For n = 1.333, normal incidence reflectance works out to about 2 percent (calculated, not quoted).
- Pavement macrotexture wavelength 0.5 to 50 mm; microtexture 1 µm to 0.5 mm (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/t504036.cfm)
- Mean texture depth: dense graded asphalt 0.4 to 0.6 mm, stone matrix asphalt over 1.0 mm, open graded friction course 1.5 to 3.0 mm (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/safety/14065/004.cfm)
- Concrete mean texture depth at least 0.8 mm; tining grooves about 3 mm wide and 3 mm deep (source: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/pavement/pubs/hif17011.pdf)
- Car park deck minimum fall 1:60. Line markings can block drainage and cause ponding (source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8124e5274a2e87db6400/enhancing_the_whole_life_structural_performance_of_multi-storey_car_parks.pdf)

### Build notes
- Darkening: multiply wet concrete albedo by about 0.5 to 0.6 and wet asphalt by about 0.6 to 0.7, then raise saturation of the base color by 10 to 20 percent. Dry asphalt base about 0.04 to 0.12 linear, concrete about 0.3 to 0.55.
- Gloss: wet porous surface roughness about 0.15 to 0.3 (film over aggregate), puddles 0.02 to 0.05, dry asphalt 0.8 to 0.9. Keep Fresnel F0 at about 0.02 for the water layer.
- Reflection length: light streaks should run toward the camera 3 to 5 times the source height (S1, S9) and be 1.5 to 2 times the source width (S3). If they come out round, the roughness is too isotropic. Stretch the lobe along the view direction or use anisotropic blur on the planar reflection.
- Texture through water: break streaks with a high frequency aggregate mask (5 to 20 mm features) that changes roughness, not only albedo, so streaks turn into speckle near the camera (S2, S6, S7) and smooth out with distance (S7).
- Color spread: large signs give wide soft blobs with no readable text (S4, S12). Small saturated lamps give narrow columns that keep their hue (S9). Blur radius should grow with roughness and distance from the surface.
- Unlit wet ground goes near black (S10, S12). Do not lift it with ambient light. Most of the read comes from reflected light.
- Painted lines: roughness about 0.4 to 0.5 when wet, albedo 0.6 to 0.8. They should stay lighter and duller than the asphalt (S4, S5, S12) and keep their own hue under colored light.
- Joints and gaps: dark lines that cut reflection columns into segments (S1, S11).
- Rain on the film: add moving ripple normals and small bright splash points inside lit areas only (S3, S10).

### Real-world sizes
- Raindrop diameter 0.1 to 9 mm mean, breaking up at large sizes. Drizzle of 0.5 mm lands at 2 m/s, 5 mm drops at about 9 m/s (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain)
- Rain rate: light under 2.5 mm/h, moderate 2.5 to 7.6 mm/h, heavy over 7.6 mm/h, violent over 50 mm/h (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain)
- Raindrops under about 2 mm are close to spherical; a 3 mm drop falls at about 8 m/s (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindrop)
- Capillary-gravity ripples on water have a minimum phase speed of about 0.23 m/s at a wavelength of about 1.7 cm (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_wave)
- Capillary length of water about 2.71 mm at 20 °C. Water bodies wider than this flatten under gravity, so a free puddle edge stands only a few mm high (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Water refractive index 1.333 (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_internal_reflection)
- Car park deck minimum fall 1:60. Ponding comes from deflection, poor falls and blocked drains; line markings can hold water (source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f8124e5274a2e87db6400/enhancing_the_whole_life_structural_performance_of_multi-storey_car_parks.pdf)

### Build notes
- Sharpness: puddle reflection roughness 0.02 to 0.05, near mirror (S2, S4, S5). The wet ground around it should be clearly blurred, 0.15 to 0.3 (S4, S6). The sharp-versus-blurred contrast at the edge is the main cue.
- Brightness: puddle reflection 70 to 100 percent of the direct source (S2, S5). Dark sky reflects black, so puddles at night read as black shapes with bright pieces of signs in them (S4, S5, S7).
- Edges: drive the puddle mask from height (low spots, joints, kerb lines) plus aggregate noise at 5 to 20 mm, so shorelines are jagged and dry stones poke through (S3, S4, S13). Allow straight edges along slab joints and line markings (S6).
- Damp halo: a darker, non-mirror band outside each puddle, about 10 to 15 percent of the puddle width (S1, S13).
- Shoreline rim: a thin bright meniscus line of 2 to 5 mm at grazing view (S5, S13).
- Ring size: with drops at 2 to 5 mm and spacing set by about 1.7 cm ripple wavelength, ring packets should reach about 2 to 15 cm diameter before fading (S12). Packets carry 2 to 6 crests (S8, S10, S11), with the largest rings about 3 to 6 times the size of new ones (S8, S11).
- Ring speed: expand outward at about 0.2 to 0.3 m/s. Lifetime about 0.3 to 0.6 s for deep water (an estimate, not measured), shorter (about half) on thin film, with 1 to 2 crests only (S9).
- Overlap: add rings as summed normals so they pass through each other without merging (S8, S11). Reflection is broken only on crests (S12).
- Density: heavy rain (over 7.6 mm/h) should give about 20 to 30 active rings in a frame-sized patch (S11). Each new ring gets a short bright or dark center dot for the rebound (S8, S11).
- Depth cues: thin water shows aggregate through it (S8, S9, S12); deep water hides it and mirrors fully. Blend the base albedo visibility by water depth over roughly 0 to 3 mm.
- Grazing view: puddles stretch to about 6:1 on screen at low camera height (S1) and rings squash to about 3:1 (S10). Fresnel should push puddle reflection near full at grazing angles.

### Build notes

- Start with 2.50 × 5.00 m stalls, 0.102 m stripe width and a 6.95 m two-way aisle. Set neighboring stall centers 2.50 m apart. Leave a clear 16.95 m wall-to-wall parking module before local islands or plant. Let the car read as deliberately angled across a line, not mistaken for a narrow stall.
- Use 0.61 m of solid concrete with the rail top at about 1.07 m above the walking surface. Repeat wall piers on the structural grid, but vary chips, pour tones, algae, and drips below post feet. Keep the rail attached to real members with visible bases and anchors.
- Give the speed ramp a 3.66 m clear lane and a 10% running slope with a softened crown and toe. Keep the barrier and drive-up reader on the flatter approach. Use a 3.5 m arm on a kerbed control island, with a dark underside strip, red reflectors and an articulated hinge housing.
- Start the lamp heads at 4.55 m and space them about 12 m apart. Use plausible paired heads and opaque housings. Break up the wet deck's highlights between pools rather than painting the whole slab one gray value.
- Model a 0.20 m square point-drain top, a near-flush grate with dark open slots and a shallow dished patch draining toward it. Set the major deck joint at 0.050 m nominal width, with a separate seal and repair shoulders. Do not render a permanent open trench from the construction photo.
- At a low camera, require visible tire scuff across an aisle end, one offset concrete patch with a distinct edge, isolated vertical parapet runoff, and a bright reflection ending at a different place from its wet patch. Give utility boxes, vent feet and stair doorway thresholds small real shadows and visible support.

### Real-world sizes
- Raindrop diameter range before aerodynamic breakup: 0.5 mm (small drizzle) to 5.0 to 5.8 mm maximum stable diameter; drops below 2.0 mm diameter stay nearly spherical while 2.0 to 5.0 mm drops flatten on the bottom into oblate hamburger buns. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Median drop diameter, fall speed, and flux by rain rate: drizzle (0.025 cm/h) has 0.96 mm median diameter, 4.1 m/s speed, 151 drops/m2/s; light rain (0.10 cm/h) has 1.24 mm, 4.8 m/s, 280 drops/m2/s; moderate rain (0.38 cm/h) has 1.60 mm, 5.7 m/s, 495 drops/m2/s; heavy rain (1.52 cm/h) has 2.05 mm, 6.7 m/s, 495 drops/m2/s; excessive rain (4.06 cm/h) has 2.40 mm, 7.3 m/s, 818 drops/m2/s; cloudburst (10.2 cm/h) has 2.85 mm, 7.9 m/s, 1220 drops/m2/s. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Sea-level terminal velocity by exact drop diameter (Gunn and Kinzer 1949, 1013 mb, 20 C): 0.5 mm falls at 2.06 m/s; 1.0 mm at 4.03 m/s; 1.6 mm at 5.65 m/s; 2.0 mm at 6.49 m/s; 2.6 mm at 7.57 m/s; 3.0 mm at 8.06 m/s; 4.0 mm at 8.83 m/s; 5.0 mm at 9.09 m/s; 5.8 mm at 9.17 m/s. (source: https://www.madsci.org/posts/archives/2000-07/962626446.Ph.r.html and https://www.atmos.albany.edu/facstaff/rfovell/ATM562/foote-dutoit-1969.pdf)
- World-space streak length against shutter time `L = v * T`: at the film configuration of 30 fps and 180 degree shutter (`T = 1/60 s = 16.67 ms`), a 1.0 mm drop (`4.03 m/s`) makes a 67 mm streak, a 1.6 mm moderate-rain drop (`5.65 m/s`) makes a 94 mm streak, a 2.0 mm heavy-rain drop (`6.49 m/s`) makes a 108 mm streak, a 3.0 mm drop (`8.06 m/s`) makes a 134 mm streak, and a 5.0 mm drop (`9.09 m/s`) makes a 152 mm streak. Projected image length is `L_px = (f_px * v * T) / z`. (source: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf)
- Pixel dwell time and streak brightness vs exposure (Garg and Nayar 2007): a falling drop crosses a point in world space in dwell time `tau = 2a / v` (with upper bound `4a / v = sqrt(a / 50) s`, always under 1.18 ms even for a 3.5 mm radius drop). Added pixel intensity over background is `Delta_I = k0 * (tau / T) * (L_r - L_b)` for near drops and `Delta_I = k0 * (4 * f_px * a^2 / (z * v * T)) * (L_r - L_b)` for far drops, so streak brightness falls as `1 / T` with exposure time and as `1 / z` with distance once streak width drops below one pixel, while lens defocus reduces streak contrast by `w / (w + b_c)` where `b_c` is the blur circle diameter. (source: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf)

### Build notes
- Lock world-space streak length to `L = v(d) * T_shutter` evaluated once at frame center time (no temporal jitter double-blurring): with `fps = 30` and `shutterDegrees = 180` (`T_shutter = 0.01667 s`), draw streaks 0.067 m long for 1.0 mm drops, 0.108 m for 2.0 mm drops, and 0.135 to 0.150 m for 3.0 to 5.0 mm drops, tilted 8 to 16 degrees from vertical along a shared wind vector with +/- 2 degrees per-drop flutter.
- Couple streak opacity strictly to local backlighting and beam intersection: raindrops refract strongly forward through a 15 to 25 degree cone (`(L_r - L_b)` term) and reflect a secondary rim glint up to ~42 degrees; over unlit sky, dark facades, and shadowed asphalt outside luminaire, headlamp, tail-lamp, or neon/LED cones, drive streak alpha below 0.01 so streaks disappear over dark areas as seen in S1, S2, S3, S4, and S6.
- Tint streaks by the local light volume they traverse: streaks within 1.5 to 3.0 m of colored neon or LED signage inherit that sign's RGB color (S4, S6), streaks inside pole luminaire cones inherit the lamp spectrum (S1, S4), and streaks inside vehicle headlamp or tail-lamp cones inherit white or red forward scatter (S2, S3).
- Near lens layer (`0.8 to 3.5 m` from camera): render sparse discrete streaks (`1.0 to 3.5 mm` diameter), widened by circle-of-confusion `b_c` and attenuated in peak alpha by `w / (w + b_c)` so close out-of-focus streaks read as soft translucent ribbons 6 to 18 px wide (with 2x horizontal anamorphic defocus oval stretch), never opaque white bars (S2, S8).
- Mid-deck layer (`3.5 to 22 m` from camera): render the main geometric streak field using a realistic drop-size mix (mostly 1.2 to 2.6 mm diameters, `90 to 130 mm` world streak lengths), modulated along each streak by subtle 15 to 30 percent intensity beading on large drops (S8) and grouped by low-frequency 3D wind-gust noise (`2 to 4 m` spatial wavelength, S7) so rain falls in moving sheets.
- Far background layer (`beyond 22 to 25 m`): stop drawing individual streak geometry once projected width `2a * f_px / z` is sub-pixel (`1/z` contrast regime); fold distant rain into the volumetric ray-marched light-cone scattering and distant skyline extinction haze (S1, S6, S7).

### Real-world sizes
- Impacting raindrop diameters and terminal velocities at sea level: 1.0 mm drops hit at 4.03 m/s; 1.6 mm (moderate rain median) at 5.65 m/s; 2.0 mm (heavy rain median) at 6.49 m/s; 3.0 mm at 8.06 m/s; 4.6 mm at ~8.95 m/s; 5.0 mm at 9.09 m/s. (source: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle and https://www.madsci.org/posts/archives/2000-07/962626446.Ph.r.html)
- Thin-film splash crown height and width (Parmentier, Terrapon, and Gilet 2023, Phys. Rev. Fluids 8, 053603, for `r_d = 2.3 mm` / `4.6 mm` diameter drops on `65 to 500 um` water films): maximum crown height is `h / r_d = 3 to 10` (`6 to 22 mm` physical height); crown top radius is `r_t / r_d = 5 to 9` (`10 to 20 mm` radius, so `20 to 40 mm` crown top width); crown base radius is `r_c / r_d = 4 to 8` (`8 to 18 mm` radius, so `16 to 36 mm` base width). Films thinner than `100 um` tear rapidly into fine mist droplets with minimal wall, while films thicker than `100 um` form coherent flared crowns. (source: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://physics.aps.org/articles/v16/86)
- Splash crown and cavity lifetime: governed by capillary timescale `t_c = sqrt(4 * rho * r_d^3 / (3 * gamma))`, which is ~15 ms for a 4.6 mm drop (peak crown height and radius reached at `14.0 +/- 1.4 ms` to `16.4 +/- 2.7 ms`). For 3.0 to 3.6 mm drops hitting water, crown height peaks at `8.9 ms`, coherent crown shape breaks down after `~12 ms`, maximum cavity depth occurs at `17.9 ms`, and primary splash structures subside by `33.9 to 35 ms` (~0.5 to 1.0 frames at 30 fps). (source: https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9103484/)
- Splash stalk and secondary bounce mist height: 2.74 mm raindrops impacting water produce a dense bright bottom splash layer averaging `14.85 mm` (~15 mm) tall, while ejected secondary micro-droplets (diameters mostly under `0.9 mm`, up to `1.8 mm`) loft a surface mist layer reaching up to `0.4 m` (40 cm) max height, with field roof photos S2 to S4 showing dense visual mist in the lowest `3 to 15 cm` and sparse ballistic droplets to `20 cm`. (source: https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10511775)
- Ring wave speed on shallow water and puddles: finite-depth gravity-capillary dispersion is `omega^2 = (g * k + (sigma / rho) * k^3) * tanh(k * h)` (`g = 9.81 m/s2`, `sigma = 0.073 N/m`, `rho = 1000 kg/m3`). In the shallow-water gravity limit (`k * h << 1`), non-dispersive wave speed is `v = sqrt(g * h)`, giving `0.14 m/s` at `h = 2 mm` depth, `0.22 m/s` at `h = 5 mm`, `0.31 m/s` at `h = 10 mm`, and `0.44 m/s` at `h = 20 mm`. Including surface tension, gravity-capillary waves have a minimum phase speed of `0.23 m/s` (23 cm/s) at `lambda = 17 mm`, while shorter `3 to 8 mm` capillary ripples travel faster at `0.30 to 0.50 m/s` (with group speed `v_g = 1.5 * v_p`), placing fine capillary ripples on the leading outside edge of each expanding ring packet. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17098v2)

### Build notes
- Damp or ultra-thin film surfaces (`< 0.1 mm` water depth, drained asphalt high spots and sloped bodywork): spawn tiny prompt-splash radial micro-fans (`8 to 15 mm` wide, `4 to 8 mm` tall) with zero expanding ring waves (S1, S5).
- Wet thin-film hard surfaces (`0.1 to 1.5 mm` water depth, flat wet concrete/asphalt, parapet coping, flat roof and hood): spawn flared conical basket crowns `16 to 36 mm` wide at the rim and `8 to 22 mm` tall, tilted `25 to 38 degrees` outward from the surface normal, with radial motion-blurred white cusp rays; because physical crown lifetime is `15 to 35 ms` against a `16.7 ms` shutter (`30 fps`, `180 degree`), render each active crown for 1 frame (occasionally decaying into a 2nd frame), never lingering across many frames (S1, S2, S4).
- Standing puddles (`> 2 mm` water depth): follow a 1-frame impact crown with a 1-frame vertical Worthington rebound stalk (`12 to 22 mm` tall, `2 to 3 mm` wide) plus an expanding concentric normal-map ring packet (S5, S6, S8).
- Drive puddle ring wave expansion at `0.22 to 0.35 m/s` radial speed (`sqrt(g * h)` bounded by the `0.23 m/s` capillary-gravity floor): spawn 3 to 5 concentric crests per impact with `8 to 16 mm` dominant wavelength (plus a subtle `4 mm` outer capillary precursor), fade ring amplitude exponentially over `0.4 to 0.8 s` so maximum ring diameter caps at `100 to 180 mm`, sum overlapping rings linearly in slope space, and ramp ring amplitude smoothly to zero where puddle depth tapers below `1.5 mm` within `1 to 2 cm` of puddle shorelines (S6).
- Add a low ground-skimming bounce-mist layer over hard horizontal surfaces where rain is heaviest (open deck bays, parapet cap, vehicle roof and rear deck): concentrate soft volumetric or card mist in the bottom `0.03 to 0.12 m` above the surface with exponential vertical falloff vanishing by `0.20 to 0.35 m`, lit strongly by grazing headlamp, tail-lamp, and low signage light (S2, S3, S4).
- Match active splash spatial density to heavy-rain flux times crown duration: at `~500 drops/m2/s` (heavy rain, `2.05 mm` median drop) and a `0.020 s` visible crown lifetime, roughly `10 active crowns per m2` exist in any single `1/60 s` frame (`~30 cm` average spacing between active crowns, matching S1 to S4), with only the largest 20 to 30 percent (`> 2.2 mm` drops) producing prominent `15 to 25 mm` baskets.

### Build notes

- Use about 12 mm neon glass on the closest sign. Build actual tube loops, dark returns, the two electrode shrouds, thin ties and standoffs. Start with the tube standing 25 to 40 mm off the painted backing as a prototype target; make attachments read in a beauty crop if the camera gets that close.
- Check the planned bright tube in an exposure bracket: a narrow pale core, colored shoulder and broad faint glow should coexist. Keep the broad glow dim enough that two 12 mm strokes separated by 30 mm do not fuse at a distance where both are resolved before the lens stage.
- Use 8 mm pitch on a cabinet grid for the main abstract wall board. Lay out the image in fixture space, but smoothly filter the emitter pattern once the projected pitch drops below roughly two output pixels. The pixel texture should never dominate a wide still or shimmer in motion.
- Make the enclosure, fascia, bolts, bracket and service gap non-emissive. Keep separate dark and active regions in the panel content, then drive the same sampled color into lighting, haze and reflections so the moving wash agrees across all three.
- Add one dead glass segment away from the main sign and one small dark block in a secondary panel, with the supports and housing still present. Let damage interrupt the source locally. Avoid wholesale random pixel noise or large halos over every light.
- Judge the wide wet frame with bloom turned off as well as on. Surface color spill, volumetric scattering and ground reflection should each remain visible without bloom; the lens pass should only add the modest fringe and soft tail.

### Build notes

- Start with three city layers: nearest facades about 20 to 60 m from the deck, middle massing around 70 to 250 m, and skyline roughly 300 to 1,200 m. They are shot-layout targets. Check that visible roof and facade edges overlap at all camera positions rather than reading as three flat backdrops.
- In front, reserve true window recess and room depth for the nearest row. Start with 1.5 m glazing modules and mix roughly 3.5 m and 4.3 m floor intervals by building type. Group lit and unlit rooms in blocks of 2 to 5 bays, then vary each lit room's level and warmth. Keep room depth and occlusion through the glass visible when the camera moves.
- Let mid-distance fixtures such as balcony units, drainpipes, planted roof masses and sign supports read as silhouettes first. Give the elevated line a real deck and end supports. Keep moving lights on that line, separated by dark gaps, with no detached points in the middle of the facades.
- Tune haze against a common neutral facade patch: as first test values, retain about 80% of clear-air contrast at 50 m, 40% at 200 m and less than 15% at 500 m for a rain-heavy viewing direction. Keep far large signs and crown lights legible after small facade detail disappears. Avoid using defocus to hide missing geometry.
- Build cloud fill as a broad low-lit field with a few unequal colored pockets. Keep most of the cloud base muted and keep the strong warm and cool sources local. In the wide shot, test for a few crown tips entering the cloud and for a clear dark gap between bright side facades.

### Real-world sizes
- Water capillary length at 20 °C: 2.71 mm. Drops wider than this are flattened by gravity into puddles; smaller drops stay near spherical caps. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Height of a large flattened puddle bead: about 2 × 2.71 mm × sin(θ/2), so about 4.2 mm at a 100° contact angle, derived from the capillary length on the same page. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Contact angle of water on commercial car waxes in one study: 71.17° to 94.85°. Hydrophobic means 90° to 150°, superhydrophobic above 150°. (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12694507/)
- Contact angle classes from a coatings maker: low repellent 70° to 80°, water repellent above 90°, a coating layer measured at 101°. Beads act as small lenses, which is why they leave water spots. (source: https://www.threebond.co.jp/en/technical/technicalnews/detail/pdf/TechnicalNews87_EN-181126.pdf)
- Bead diameters seen on real car paint: about 0.3 mm to 6 mm for single beads, 10 to 20 mm for merged puddle beads. (estimates from S3 and S4 photos: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raindrops_on_a_well-polished_car_roof.jpg and https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_drops_on_car_roof.jpg)

### Build notes
- Bead shape: spherical cap with contact angle 95° to 110° for waxed or coated paint. Above 2.7 mm diameter, flatten the top so height caps near 4 mm.
- Size spread: use a power law or two lobes. About 70 percent of beads 0.3 to 1.5 mm, 25 percent 1.5 to 6 mm, 5 percent merged 8 to 20 mm. Tiny beads fill gaps at 1 to 2 diameters spacing.
- Merged beads: union of 2 to 4 overlapping circles with a soft min blend, so lobes stay visible. Never a clean ellipse.
- Refraction: bead body shows the surface under it shifted and darker than the dry paint (about 0.6 to 0.8 of paint luminance on colored paint). On light paint draw a dark crescent on the side away from the key light; on black paint the body is near black.
- Highlights: one sharp specular point or short arc per bead, on the side toward the light, 5 to 15 percent of bead radius in size. On black paint at distance, beads become only these points.
- Overcast or soft signage light: widen the highlight to a soft band, drop contrast about half.
- Grazing view: squash beads 3 to 4 to 1 and put a bright rim along the far edge.
- Trails: on slopes above about 10°, give large beads a teardrop with tail pointing uphill. Draw sliding paths as clear lanes 1 to 2 bead widths wide with a string of 0.2 to 0.5 mm beads along the edges.
- Pooling: increase density 1.5 to 2 times along gutters, roof rails and lower panel edges.
- Depth of field: at macro framing, keep the sharp band to about 20 percent of frame height; out of focus beads become soft color blobs with a faint pale highlight.

- Conventional windshield: two 2.1 mm soda-lime glass plies with a 0.76 mm PVB interlayer; lighter builds use 2.1 mm outer and 1.6 mm inner. Typical windshield area about 1.4 m². (source: https://www.corning.com/microsites/ags/downloads/white-papers/novel-lightweight-laminate-concept-for-automotive-windshields.pdf)

- Typical raindrop diameter: 0.5 to 5 mm. Drops slide down a vertical pane when larger than about 2.7 mm; smaller drops may stick until another drop adds mass. (source: https://hal.science/hal-02343291v1/file/SlidingDropsToledano.pdf)
- Raindrops are near spherical below 1 mm, flattened above, and cannot exceed about 10 mm. (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindrop_size_distribution)

### Build notes
- Two layers: static drops (0.5 to 3 mm, round or teardrop, density 1 drop per 2 to 4 drop areas) and moving rivulets (width 1 to 2 mm, spacing 2 to 4 percent of view width on a side window).
- Rivulet path: mostly downward on a parked car, with lateral wander of 1 to 3 widths every 3 to 6 widths of travel. On a moving car tilt paths up to about 30° to 45° from vertical along the air flow (S8).
- Start sliding only for drops above 2.7 mm equivalent; give sliding drops a rounded front and pointed rear, and leave a string of 0.2 to 0.5 mm drops in the wake.
- Later drops should follow earlier tracks most of the time; new tracks only occasionally.
- Refraction: offset the background inside each drop by 2 to 6 percent of drop radius times drop curvature, and invert it inside drops over 2 mm. Add a dark rim 10 to 20 percent of radius on the side away from the brightest light.
- Water film: low frequency normal noise, amplitude about 1 to 2 percent of view width in background offset, animated at a few cycles per second in heavy rain. Smears lights downward.
- Fog or condensation: optional uniform veil raising blacks 3 to 6 percent with clear lanes where rivulets passed.
- Defocus: with focus past the glass, render lit drops as discs of fixed size per lens, onion texture on the disc, near black between. With anamorphic squeeze apply the same vertical oval as the rest of the frame.
- Drops are only visible where something bright is behind or reflected; keep drops in black areas close to invisible.

### Real-world sizes
- Condensed steam droplet diameter vs raindrop diameter: condensing vapor jets (`T_s = 106 to 172 C` mixing into cooler air) nucleate liquid water micro-droplets `0.5 to 22 um` in diameter (droplets under `50 um` follow turbulent Gaussian air dispersion with negligible fall speed), roughly 100 to 500 times smaller than `1.0 to 5.0 mm` (`1000 to 5000 um`) raindrops falling ballistically at `4.03 to 9.09 m/s`. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14787 and https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0736/ML073620431.pdf and https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle)
- Steam jet-to-plume hydrodynamics and entrainment: near a pressurized vent of radius `R_s`, flow starts as a momentum-dominated jet scaling with `R_s` (typical industrial/plant stack exit speeds `1.0 to 10.9 m/s`, low-pressure drain vents `0.3 to 1.2 m/s`) before transitioning at crossover height `z_c` to a buoyancy-dominated plume whose width and height scale with mass flux as `m_dot^(2/5)` and whose horizontal ambient-air entrainment velocity equals `0.3` times local vertical rise speed (giving a ~14 to 16 degree half-angle cone in calm air, matching S2). Cool, high-humidity rain air (`RH > 90 percent`) slows droplet re-evaporation and extends visible steam plume length by 2 to 4 times compared with dry air. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14787 and https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0736/ML073620431.pdf)
- Air refractive index temperature gradient for heat shimmer (Ciddor equation at `550 nm`): `n(550 nm, T) = 1 + 2.778e-4 * (288.15 K / T)`, yielding `dn / dT = -0.96e-6 K^-1` near `15 C` (about `1.0e-5` refractive index drop per `10 K` air heating, or `~1.0e-4` for a `100 K` hot exhaust plume). Over warm horizontal surfaces, air temperature decays exponentially as `T(y) = T_cool + Delta_T * exp(-y / beta)` with thermal boundary layer thickness `beta = 0.3 to 2.0 cm` over pavement and `5.0 cm` over warm water. (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09131v1)
- Reference raindrop, streak, splash, and wave scales interacting with steam on the deck: raindrops `1.0 to 5.0 mm` (`4.03 to 9.09 m/s`) trace `67 to 152 mm` streaks at `1/60 s` shutter; hard-surface splash crowns span `16 to 40 mm` width and `6 to 22 mm` height over a `15 to 35 ms` lifetime; puddle rings propagate at `0.14 to 0.44 m/s` (`sqrt(g * h)` for `2 to 20 mm` depths, bounded by the `0.23 m/s` capillary-gravity minimum). (sources: https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/old/publications/pdfs/Garg_IJCV07.pdf and https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/303206/1/PhysRevFluids.8.053603.pdf and https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17098v2)

### Build notes
- Deck drain grate steam (`low momentum, v_exit = 0.4 to 0.9 m/s`): emit 4 to 6 separate clumpy ribbons (`6 to 15 cm` initial width, 30 to 50 percent clear gaps at the grate rim, S4) that creep `0.3 to 0.8 m` horizontally along the wet deck with the wind (`0.2 to 0.4 m` layer height, S1, S5) before lifting at `25 to 40 degrees` and fading completely by `1.8 to 3.0 m` height.
- Rooftop plant and HVAC flue steam (`moderate momentum, v_exit = 1.5 to 3.5 m/s` from a `0.25 to 0.40 m` vent): leave a `5 to 10 cm` semi-transparent condensation gap right at the hot metal lip (S2), bend the jet `45 to 60 degrees` into the shared wind direction (same wind vector that tilts the rain `8 to 16 degrees`), expand cone width at a `15 degree` half-angle (`0.3x` entrainment ratio) with `0.3 to 0.6 m` Kelvin-Helmholtz upper-edge billows, and fade optical depth smoothly to zero over `3.5 to 6.0 m` travel (S2, S6).
- Forward-peaked Mie scattering in steam (`0.5 to 22 um` droplets, `Henyey-Greenstein g = 0.65 to 0.80`): when a vehicle lamp, luminaire, or bright neon/LED sign sits behind the steam within a `20 to 35 degree` forward cone, boost scattered radiance 3 to 5 times over side-lit steam and tint that local pocket with the emitter's exact color while radially blurring sharp background edges behind optical depths `0.5 to 2.0` (S1, S2, S3).
- Beer-Lambert extinction and dark-zone falloff: more than `3 m` away from lights in front of dark sky or unlit facades, drop steam luminance below `8 to 12 percent` so unlit vapor never self-glows at night (S6), and let dense foreground wisps (`optical depth > 1.0`) crossing directly in front of bright signage slightly dim the sign face by out-scattering extinction (S5).
- Keep heat shimmer dry, subtle, and strictly localized in rain: do not apply road-mirage shimmer across the rain-cooled wet deck; place a small 3D transparent volume only over active hot exhausts (vehicle rear exhaust outlets if warm, and the first `0.15 to 1.5 m` above rooftop boiler or plant hot flues) with zero opacity/fog and a screen-space UV refraction offset of `1 to 4 px` at `1080p` driven by `5 to 15 cm` upward-advecting curl-noise eddies at `1.5 to 3.0 m/s` rise speed, partially motion-softened for the `1/60 s` shutter (S7, S8, S9).

#### S7 · Cooke Optics, what anamorphic lenses do

- Observations:

### Build notes
- Exposure: key the car and subject around 1.5 to 2.3 stops under middle grey (scene linear about 0.04 to 0.06 if mid grey is 0.18); let backgrounds sit 2 to 4 stops lower again.
- Black: lift the darkest shadow to about 2 to 4 percent display (not 0) and keep texture visible on asphalt and under trees. True 0 only under the car and in the deepest gaps. Blacks neutral or slightly green-cool, never saturated.
- Color: neutral dark grey for structure; saturation only in signs, tail lamps and their reflections. Practical street light colors: sodium amber and mercury green, not pure blue.
- Paint on a dark car: show the body through 1 to 3 long highlight streaks from a large soft source above (a flown bounce), not through general fill.
- Lens: default 2x anamorphic look on 35 to 40 mm equivalent for coverage, 18 to 24 mm for close and low car shots, stop T2 to T2.8.
- Bokeh: vertical ovals, width 0.5 of height for 2x (0.67 for 1.5x). Disc edge slightly brighter; size scales with defocus. Apply the same oval to rain drops on the lens.
- Flare: horizontal streak only for the strongest 1 to 3 sources (headlights, the brightest sign), length 30 to 100 percent of frame width, thickness 0.2 to 0.5 percent of frame height, cool blue tint, intensity a few percent of source. Add 2 to 4 faint elliptical ghosts on the line through frame center. No streaks for ordinary signage.
- Halation and bloom: soft warm bloom around the brightest highlights only, radius 0.5 to 2 percent of frame height.
- Lens water: an optional layer of fixed drops on the lens, fewer drops when a headlight points into the lens.
- Moving light: for interior or close shots, animate passing light bands across the car at road speed.

### Real-world sizes
- Water contact angle on galvanized steel: about 62 degrees (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251997/table/polymers-17-01796-t008/)
- Water contact angle on stainless steel: about 70.8 degrees (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3769772/)
- Water contact angle on natural rubber vulcanizate: about 103.6 degrees (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11644439/); about 107 degrees in another study (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13665833/)
- Hot-dip galvanized coating: minimum average about 85 µm, up to about 140 µm, often with a spangle finish (source: https://steelconstruction.info/topics/corrosion-protection/metallic-coatings)
- Reactive steels galvanize to a matte gray, mottled finish (source: https://galvanizeit.org/uploads/publications/Galvanized_Steel_Inspection_Guide.pdf)
- Tyre tread depth new about 8 mm; legal minimum 1.6 mm (source: https://www.halfords.com/tyres/advice/tread-depth-and-safety.html)
- Capillary length of water about 2.71 mm at 20 °C, which caps how tall a large drop can stand before it flattens (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_length)
- Raindrops 0.5 to 4 mm typical; under about 2 mm near spherical (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raindrop)

### Build notes
- Rubber dry: albedo about 0.04 to 0.06 linear (25 to 30 percent sRGB value, S3), roughness 0.75 to 0.9. Wet with film: albedo about 0.02 to 0.03, roughness 0.2 to 0.3 on exposed faces (knob tops, rib tops) so they get the black lacquer look (S2). Grooves stay rougher. Beads 1 to 6 mm on flat rubber (S1), from a contact angle above 100 degrees.
- Galvanized steel: metallic, base about 0.6 to 0.7 gray, roughness 0.4 to 0.55, plus a spangle or mottle mask at 5 to 30 mm scale. Wet: drops are flat (contact angle about 62 degrees), 1 to 6 mm, drawn darker than the zinc with a light rim (S2, S10). Do not drop the base roughness much; the zinc stays frosted.
- Stainless and brushed steel: wet shows as a sheet with holes (S13) or flattened drops up to about 15 mm, flat topped above about 5 mm (S14). Keep the anisotropic grain visible through the water.
- Gloss painted steel: dry roughness 0.25 to 0.4. Wet: dense beads of 0.5 to 4 mm on horizontal faces (S7, S11), teardrops about 2:1 on vertical faces (S4, S6), and a row of pendant drops on bottom edges at 1 to 2 drop widths spacing (S5, S6, S7). Base color under drops about 10 to 20 percent darker and more saturated (S4).
- Weathered or chipped paint: water forms flat pools with meniscus lines, not beads (S8). Use the chip mask to switch bead mode to pool mode.
- Rust: matte (roughness about 0.85 to 0.95) and darker when wet, with no beading (S4). Darken albedo about 30 percent wet and leave roughness high.
- Chrome: keep roughness near 0.05; drops show as dark-ringed lenses that break the mirror into speckle where dense (S5, S11).
- Cast iron: wet value about 20 percent (S12), sheen on flat tops, raised edges catching the sky.
- Every drop: bright point highlight, dark rim, bright caustic on the side away from the light (S1, S9, S14). At distance, replace drops with a speckle mask in the normal and roughness maps.
- Wetness kinds by material: beads on rubber and gloss paint, flat drops on galvanized steel, sheet on stainless and porous concrete, none on rust (it darkens only).

### Build notes
- Slider: travel 0.3 to 1.0 m, speed 0.02 to 0.13 m/s, move length 6 to 15 s. Ease in and out over the first and last 15 to 25 percent of the move. Lens 35 to 85 mm equivalent; camera 0.3 to 1.2 m from the car for detail (badges, beads, light edge).
- Dolly: travel 1.8 to 3 m on short track, speed about 0.2 to 0.6 m/s, move length 4 to 12 s (derived ranges from track length and a slow push, not published speeds). Boom range up to 1.09 m during the move. Camera height 0.3 to 1.2 m; lens 24 to 40 mm.
- Telescopic crane: telescope at 0.3 to 1.5 m/s for smooth work, cap 2 m/s; full extension 3 to 12 m takes 3 to 6 s minimum, so a hero crane move should be 6 to 15 s. Height 0.3 to 7 m. Ease in and out each 1 to 2 s; avoid harder stops, the specs single out deflection at starts and stops.
- Arm car: camera 0.3 to 1.5 m above road, 2 to 4.3 m from the car centre line, swing at 10 to 60° per second (tip speed up to about 4.5 m/s on top of road speed). Lens 18 to 40 mm. Keep frames settled: no shake, slow orbit while matching the car.
- Robot arm: for whip moves use up to 2 to 4 m/s with ease of about 0.25 s each end (from 1 m in 0.5 s). Use only for very short, fast beats.
- Easing curve: smooth S curve (cubic or quintic). No maker publishes ease times; use 15 to 25 percent of move length per end for slow moves, and at least 1 s per end for crane and arm moves over 3 m.
- Pan and tilt on top of a move: 2 to 15° per second for calm shots. Gimbal limits (360° per second and up) are not a guide.
- Framing defaults for this car: low (0.3 to 0.6 m) and close with 24 to 40 mm for hero shots; 18 mm only for top shots and very close passes; never shake the frame to sell speed.
- Repeat passes: plan moves as keyframed paths with fixed timing so light and reflections line up on each take.
